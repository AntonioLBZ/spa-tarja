# Invitations and nicknames

The decision is in [ADR-0014](../adr/0014-people-in-groups.md). Everything here needs a connection ([offline sync](offline-sync.md)). Friends, adding people to a group and merging a dummy are in [groups](groups.md); deleting an account is in [authentication](authentication.md).

## Rules

- **Nickname** (migration `0010`). A user may set one, trimmed, of 1 to 30 characters counted in UTF-16 code units (an emoji counts 2), with at least one that shows: a nickname made only of invisible characters (zero-width spaces and joiners, direction marks and the other format characters, `\p{Cf}`, and the rest of the default ignorable ones) is refused, by the same check in the app and on the server. It is not unique and nobody can be found by it: it is only the name their friends see. The table `profile` has one row per user who set one (`user_id`, `nickname`, `updated_at`); removing it deletes the row. Without one, the name is that of the Google account. `GET /profile` answers `{ nickname, name }` (the name of the account is there so the screen can show what is used instead) and `PUT /profile` with `{ nickname }` (null removes it) answers the same, or `400`.
- **Where it shows.** In a friend, the other person is shown by their nickname when they have one, live, and by the name stored on their member otherwise. A person who makes a group, or who is added to one, is named there after their nickname, else the name of their Google account, as it is at that moment; from then on the name in that group is the group's ([groups](groups.md)). The email is never shown.
- **An invitation is a link, and it is for being friends.** There are no invitations to a group and no way to look a person up. A friend is made first, by name, as a dummy next to the maker ([groups](groups.md)); the invitation is for that dummy, and whoever opens the link and accepts takes its place, becomes the friend and keeps everything in the ledger between the two.
- **The table** `friend_invitations` (migration `0012`, [data model](data-model.md)): `id` (a UUID made by the server), `group_id` (the friend, a `direct` group), `member_id` (its placeholder), `created_by`, `created_at`, `expires_at` (14 days after), when it was accepted, declined or revoked, and who accepted it (who declined or revoked is not kept: nothing reads it). A friend has at most one open invitation: making another revokes the first.
- **The token** of the link is `<id>.<signature>`: an HMAC-SHA256 of the id under `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`, in base64url, which is never stored, so the table alone (a leak, a backup) opens no link, while the server can still list the open link again for Copy. A token is checked by its shape, then by its signature (`crypto.subtle.verify`), before anything is read. Changing the secret voids every open link, as it ends every session.
- **Making one**: `POST /groups/:friendId/invitations` makes it; the link is `<origin>/join/<token>`. Any member of the friend can, while the other side is still a dummy and the friend is open: otherwise `409 closed`, `409 not-a-friend` for a group, and `409 not-placeholder` once the other side is a person. `GET /groups/:friendId/invitations` reads the invitation made last, and `DELETE /groups/:friendId/invitations` revokes the open one (sent again, or for a friend that is closed or a person, it changes nothing). The three answer `{ invitation }`: `{ state: 'open', token, expiresAt }` while it can be used (the token too, so the link can be copied again), `{ state: 'declined' }`, or null (none, revoked, expired, accepted, or the friend closed). They are routes of the friend, so whoever isn't in it gets the `404` of a group that doesn't exist ([groups](groups.md)), and only its members ever read a token.
- **Receiving one**: the token goes in a JSON body, `{ token }`, never in the URL of a request, since the Worker's request logs keep URLs. `POST /invitations/read` shows it to whoever has the link while signed in: `{ name }`, the name of who asks (their nickname, else the name of their account, as a group would start them), and nothing else. `POST /invitations/accept` makes the caller the member (`user_id`, `claimed_at`) and answers the friend as `GET /groups` lists it: the dummy keeps its id, so every entry and balance is the person's and nothing is rewritten, and the label the maker typed gives way to the caller's nickname or the name of their account, so the friend is shown by the person's own name from then on. Sent again by the same person, it answers the friend again. `POST /invitations/decline` closes it (`204`), and the person who made it reads it as declined. Answers: `410 gone`, always with the same body, when it doesn't exist, isn't a token of the right shape and signature, expired, was revoked, used or declined, the dummy was claimed, or the friend is closed or its other side no longer a user; then `409 yourself` when the caller is the other side of the friend (to read and to decline too); and, to accept, `409 already-friends` when the caller is already a friend of that person through another open group of two, and `409 too-many-friends` over the limit of 100. Accepting marks the invitation and claims the dummy in one batch, and the mark is made only while the invitation can be used and the two aren't friends, read in that same statement, so a link serves one person, and two links of one person accepted at the same moment make one friend.
- **Duplicates.** The same person can end up as a dummy friend that someone typed and as a real friend that came through a link of another dummy. Two friends are never merged. The way to avoid it is to send the link of the dummy that exists.
- **Sign-in from a link.** Opening `/join/<token>` signed out shows the sign-in screen, with a line that says signing in shows the invitation, and the Google round trip comes back to that same address: `startSignIn` sends the current path as the `callbackURL` when it is exactly `/join/` and a token of the right shape, and `/` otherwise ([authentication](authentication.md)). Nothing else is ever sent as the way back.
- **Not guessable, not forever**: a token that isn't found, is forged, expired, was revoked or is used answers the same `410`, so a token can't be told from a missing one. A working token needs a signature of 256 bits under a secret the database doesn't hold, so probing tokens is not a way in, and every route that takes one needs a session. **No rate limit** is configured for them in Cloudflare (decided with this work): guessing can't succeed at any rate, the join page is a static file the Worker never runs for, and each guess costs the prober a request with a Google session, so a rule would only slow down what can't work. Should the API ever need shielding from sheer load, that is a rule for all of `/api/*`, not one for invitations.

## Screen

- **Settings → Profile** (a section after the language, the theme and the app color): a `Field` for the nickname with the hint "Your friends see this name. It doesn't have to be unique. Until you set one, they see the name of your Google account, <name>.", the `name` of `GET /profile`, Save and, once there is one, Remove. Saving the field empty removes it too; a nickname with nothing visible in it is refused under the field and not sent. Offline the field and its buttons are off with a notice.
- **Invite**, in the edit screen of an open friend whose other side is a dummy, after Show in my expenses: a `Sheet` labeled "Invite" with a hint (whoever opens the link and accepts takes the place of the dummy and sees what was shared; it works once, for 14 days) and a ghost button "Make an invitation link". Once there is an open link the sheet shows it instead, in a read-only field "Invitation link" with the hint "Open until 15 Nov. It works once.", and two ghost buttons: Share link, the share sheet of the phone where there is one (`navigator.share`), else Copy link, which copies it and says so; and Revoke link. The link is made first and sent after, since a copy or a share that waits for the network can be refused for want of a press of the person's own; a link that couldn't be copied or shared says to copy it by hand from the field. When the latest was declined the sheet says "The invitation was declined." with "Make another link". The focus goes to the field once a link is made, and to the button once it is revoked. Offline the buttons are off, like the rest of the screen. The form of Add a friend opens the new friend ([groups](groups.md)), from whose settings the link is made.
- **`/join/$token`** (depth 1, under Groups in the header and the bar): the title "Friend invitation" and a sheet labeled with the name of who asks, which says "<Name> wants to be your friend." and what being friends brings, with Accept and Decline as ghost buttons. Accepting opens the friend, in place of the link in the history. Declining says so, with the link to Groups. A link that is gone, or the person's own, says so in a `Notice` with the link to Groups; a refusal to accept (already friends, too many friends) is said under the sheet, and a link that couldn't be read says so with Try again. Offline the page says it needs a connection and asks nothing until the connection is back; Accept and Decline are off.
- **The link stays on the page.** The page is a static file the Worker never sees, and the app sends only its origin as the referrer (`<meta name="referrer" content="strict-origin">` in `index.html`), so neither another site nor the requests of the app carry the path of the link.
