# Invitations and nicknames

The decision is in [ADR-0014](../adr/0014-people-in-groups.md), amended at the owner's request (2026-11-06) so that a friend who uses the app is invited directly, with no dummy first, and so that a friend exists only from an accepted link: nobody is made a friend by typing a name, and a dummy lives only inside a group. Everything here needs a connection ([offline sync](offline-sync.md)). Friends, adding people to a group and merging a dummy are in [groups](groups.md); deleting an account is in [authentication](authentication.md).

## Rules

- **Nickname** (migration `0010`). A user may set one, trimmed, of 1 to 30 characters counted in UTF-16 code units (an emoji counts 2), with at least one that shows: a nickname made only of invisible characters (zero-width spaces and joiners, direction marks and the other format characters, `\p{Cf}`, and the rest of the default ignorable ones) is refused, by the same check in the app and on the server. So is one with a control that overrides or isolates the direction of the text (U+202A to U+202E, U+2066 to U+2069), which could reverse the rest of the line a name sits in; the plain marks (U+200E, U+200F), which text pasted from right-to-left scripts often carries, are kept among visible characters. The same holds for the name of a group and for the name a group gives a person (`400`), and the name of a Google account loses all of them, marks included, when it becomes the name a person starts with in a group. The refusal says that a name needs a visible character and no text-direction controls. It is not unique and nobody can be found by it: it is only the name their friends see. The table `profile` has one row per user who set one (`user_id`, `nickname`, `updated_at`); removing it deletes the row. Without one, the name is that of the Google account. `GET /profile` answers `{ nickname, name }` (the name of the account is there so the screen can show what is used instead) and `PUT /profile` with `{ nickname }` (null removes it) answers the same, or `400`.
- **Where it shows.** In a friend, the other person is shown by their nickname when they have one, live, and by the name stored on their member otherwise. A person who makes a group, or who is added to one, is named there after their nickname, else the name of their Google account, as it is at that moment; from then on the name in that group is the group's ([groups](groups.md)). The email is never shown.
- **An invitation is a link, and it is for being friends.** There are no invitations to a group and no way to look a person up. It is made on the Groups tab with nobody behind it: whoever opens the link and accepts becomes a new friend of who made it. It is the only way to make a friend: there is no invitation for a dummy, as a friend has none, and the server has no route to make or read one.
- **The table** `friend_invitations` (migration `0012`, [data model](data-model.md)): `id` (a UUID made by the server), `group_id` (null until it is accepted, when it holds the friend it made), `created_by`, `created_at`, `expires_at` (14 days after), when it was accepted, declined or revoked, and who accepted it (who declined or revoked is not kept: nothing reads it). A person has at most 5 open invitations (`MAX_OPEN_INVITATIONS`).
- **The token** of the link is `<id>.<signature>`: an HMAC-SHA256 of the id under `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`, in base64url. The signature is never stored in the invitation tables, so a copy of them (a leak, a backup) opens no link, while the server can still sign the open link again for Copy. The link does travel in the `callbackURL` of a sign-in started from it (below), which Better Auth keeps in its `verification` table for at most 10 minutes, and for longer if the sign-in is abandoned, until that record is cleaned up. A token is checked by its shape, then by its signature (`crypto.subtle.verify`), before anything is read. The shape allows one spelling of the signature only: the last of its 43 characters carries 4 bits of the 6 it could, so four spellings would decode to the same bytes, and only the one the server writes is a link. The same invitation is thus one link, never four. Changing the secret voids every open link, as it ends every session.
- **Making one**: `POST /invitations`, with no body, makes it and answers `{ invitation: { id, token, expiresAt } }`; the link is `<origin>/join/<token>`. It is refused with `409 too-many-invitations` when the caller has 5 open invitations, counted in the statement that adds it, so makes at the same moment can't pass it (revoking one, or its end, frees the place), and with `409 too-many-friends` or `409 too-many-closed-groups` when the caller can't take another friend. A make sent again after an answer that never came makes a second link, listed with the first. Making a link also deletes, in the same batch, the caller's own invitations that were never accepted and ended more than 30 days ago (`ENDED_INVITATION_DAYS`): the end is the day it was revoked or declined, or else the day it expired. So the table doesn't grow with every link made and revoked. An invitation that was accepted, another person's, and one that ended less than 30 days ago are never touched, so revoking a link just revoked still answers `200`. `GET /invitations` answers `{ invitations }`: the caller's open invitations, newest first, each with its token, so the link can be copied again; one that was used, declined, revoked or has expired is not there. `DELETE /invitations/:invitationId` revokes one and answers the list; revoking one that is over changes nothing; an id that is not an invitation of the caller (another person's, or one that doesn't exist) is `404 not-found`, the same answer for all, so nobody learns whether an id is in use. Every answer of `/invitations` has `Cache-Control: no-store`, as it can carry a link or a token.
- **Receiving one**: the token goes in a JSON body, `{ token }`, never in the URL of a request, since the Worker's request logs keep URLs. The body must be a JSON object with a `token` key, or the answer is `400`; what the token is, of any type, shape or length, is read afterwards and answers as below. `POST /invitations/read` shows it to whoever has the link while signed in: `{ name }`, the name of who made it (their nickname, else the name of their account, as a group would start them), and nothing else. `POST /invitations/accept` makes the friend: a `direct` group in the app's currency, made by who invited, with two members who are users, who invited as the owner and then the caller, each named after their nickname else the name of their account. The ids of the group and its members are made by the server, as making a friend needs a connection anyway. It answers the friend as `GET /groups` lists it, and sent again by the same person it answers the friend again.
- `POST /invitations/decline` closes it (`204`) and it leaves the list of its maker. Answers: `410 gone`, always with the same body, when it doesn't exist, isn't a string or a token of the right shape and signature, expired, was revoked, used or declined, or the person who made it deleted their account; then `409 yourself` when the caller is who asks (to read and to decline too); and, to accept, `409 already-friends` when the caller is already a friend of that person through an open group of two, `409 too-many-friends` over the limit of 100, `409 too-many-closed-groups` when the caller has 20 closed groups and `409 inviter-at-limit` when who asks has 100 friends or 20 closed groups (one answer for both, so it tells no more than that they can't take a friend now). The limits are counted just before, as those of making a group. Accepting is one batch, which D1 runs as one transaction. "Can be taken" is one condition, read inside the statement that uses it: the invitation can be used, the caller isn't who asks and the two aren't friends. The group is written first, only while the invitation can be taken; the mark is the second statement, under the same condition; and the two members are the only statements guarded by the mark, each written only if it is this request's. So a link serves one person, two people accepting it at the same moment make one friend and leave no half-made group, and two links of one person accepted at the same moment make one friend.
- **Sign-in from a link.** Opening `/join/<token>` signed out shows the sign-in screen, with a line that says signing in shows the invitation, and the Google round trip comes back to that same address, also when it fails (with `?signin=failed`, which adds the failed notice to the invitation line): `startSignIn` sends the current path as the `callbackURL` when it is exactly `/join/` and a token of the right shape, and `/` otherwise ([authentication](authentication.md)). Nothing else is ever sent as the way back. It is the only way for a sign-in that leaves the installed app and comes back in another browser context to end on the link, so it stays, with the limit on how long Better Auth keeps it.
- **Not guessable, not forever**: a token that isn't found, is forged, expired, was revoked or is used answers the same `410`, so a token can't be told from a missing one. A working token needs a signature of 256 bits under a secret the database doesn't hold, so probing tokens is not a way in, and every route that takes one needs a session. **No rate limit** is configured for them in Cloudflare (decided with this work): guessing can't succeed at any rate, the join page is a static file the Worker never runs for, and each guess costs the prober a request with a Google session, so a rule would only slow down what can't work. Should the API ever need shielding from sheer load, that is a rule for all of `/api/*`, not one for invitations.

## Screen

- **Settings → Profile** (a section after the language, the theme and the app color): a `Field` for the nickname with the hint "Your friends see this name. It doesn't have to be unique. Until you set one, they see the name of your Google account, <name>.", the `name` of `GET /profile`, Save and, once there is one, Remove. Saving the field empty removes it too; a nickname with nothing visible in it is refused under the field and not sent. Offline the field and its buttons are off with a notice.
- **Invite a friend**, on the Groups tab ([groups](groups.md)): a primary `block` button at the top of the Friends section, under its heading, with a hint under it: the link is for somebody who uses the app or will, they become a friend when they accept, and it works once for 14 days. Pressing it makes an invitation, and the links of the person that are still open are on a `Sheet` labeled "Invitation links" under the hint, newest first, each the read-only field "Invitation link" with "Open until 15 Nov. It works once." and two ghost buttons: Share link, the share sheet of the phone where there is one (`navigator.share`), else Copy link, which copies it and says so; and Revoke link. The link is made first and sent after, since a copy or a share that waits for the network can be refused for want of a press of the person's own; a link that couldn't be copied or shared says to copy it by hand from the field. A new link takes the focus; once one is revoked the focus goes back to Invite a friend. Over the limit of open links the refusal is said in words under the button ("You have 5 open invitation links, the most there can be. Revoke one first."). Offline the button and the links' buttons are off, like the rest of the tab, whose notice says that inviting a friend needs a connection too. The list is read when the tab opens and isn't kept on the device; when it can't be read a notice says so, and a link made then is listed alone while the list is read again. A link that was accepted leaves the list, and the friend is in the list of friends once the groups are read again.
- **`/join/$token`** (depth 1, under Groups in the header and the bar): the title "Friend invitation" and a sheet labeled with the name of who asks, which says "<Name> wants to be your friend." and what accepting does: that the two become friends, share expenses and see what they owe each other, and that their part of those expenses counts in their own expenses and can be turned off with Show in my expenses. Accept and Decline are ghost buttons. Accepting opens the friend, in place of the link in the history. Declining says so, with the link to Groups. A link that is gone, or the person's own, says so in a `Notice` with the link to Groups; a refusal to accept (already friends, too many friends or closed groups, who asks can't take more friends) is said under the sheet, and a link that couldn't be read says so with Try again. Offline the page says it needs a connection and asks nothing until the connection is back; Accept and Decline are off.
- **The link stays on the page.** The page is a static file the Worker never sees, and the app sends only its origin as the referrer (`<meta name="referrer" content="strict-origin">` in `index.html`, and the same policy as a response header, [authentication](authentication.md)), so neither another site nor the requests of the app carry the path of the link.
