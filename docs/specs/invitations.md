# People and invitations

The decision is in [ADR-0014](../adr/0014-people-in-groups.md). Everything here needs a connection ([offline sync](offline-sync.md)). Friends, adding people to a group and merging a dummy are in [groups](groups.md); deleting an account is in [authentication](authentication.md).

## Rules

- **Username** (migration `0010`). A user may set one, 3 to 30 characters of `a-z`, `0-9`, `.`, `_` and `-`, stored in lowercase; capitals typed are lowercased. It is unique among users. The table `profile` has one row per user who set one; removing it deletes the row, so "no row" is the default. The handle of a user is the username when there is one and the email of their account otherwise. Nobody sees another user's username or email in a group: groups show the name of the member.
- **Looking someone up** (`GET /people/lookup?handle=`) takes a handle, an exact match ignoring case, and answers `{ name, you }` where `name` is the name of the Google account, or `404`. It never matches a part of a handle, and it answers only to a signed-in user. The email is matched when it has an `@`, the username otherwise; an account with a username set is not found by its email. Nothing else about the account is answered.
- **Profile API**: `GET /profile` answers `{ username, handle }` for the caller; `PUT /profile` with `{ username }` (null removes it) answers the profile, `409 username-taken` or `400`.
- **An invitation is for being friends.** There are no invitations to a group. A friend is made first, by name, as a dummy next to the maker ([groups](groups.md)); an invitation is for that dummy, and whoever accepts takes its place, becomes the friend and keeps everything in the ledger between the two. Table `friend_invitations` (migration `0012`): `token` (random, 128 bits, in the link), `group_id` (the friend), `member_id` (its placeholder), `target_user_id` (null for a link, else the user it is for), `created_by`, `created_at`, `expires_at` (14 days after), and when it was accepted, declined or revoked. A friend has at most one open invitation: making another revokes the first. There are two ways to make one:
    - **A link**, for whoever receives it: `POST /groups/:friendId/invitations` with `{}`.
    - **A handle**: `{ handle }` makes it for that user, who sees it in their Groups screen; `404` when nobody has the handle, `409 already-friends` when a friend of the caller is already that user, and `409 yourself`.
- **Making one**: any member of the friend, while the other side is still a dummy and the group is open. It answers `{ token, expiresAt }`; the link is `<origin>/join/<token>`. `GET /groups/:friendId/invitations` lists the open one (the token too, so the link can be copied again) and `DELETE /invitations/:token` revokes it.
- **Receiving one**: `GET /invitations` lists the open ones made for the caller's handle, with the name of who asks. `GET /invitations/:token` shows an invitation to whoever has the link while signed in: who asks, and nothing else. `POST /invitations/:token/accept` with an optional `{ name }` makes the caller the member (`user_id`, `claimed_at`), who keeps the name the dummy had unless the body renames it; `POST /invitations/:token/decline` closes it. Answers: `410 gone` when it doesn't exist, expired, was revoked, used or declined, the dummy left or was merged, the friend is closed, or it was made for another user; `409 already-friends` when the caller is already a friend of that person through another group of two, or is the maker; `409 too-many-friends` over the limit. Accepting is atomic with marking the invitation, so a link serves one person.
- **Duplicates.** The same person can end up as a dummy friend that someone typed and as a real friend that came by their own invitation. Two friends are never merged. The way to avoid it is to send the invitation of the dummy that exists, which is why every invitation is for one.
- **Sign-in from a link.** Opening `/join/<token>` signed out shows the sign-in screen, and the Google round trip comes back to that same address: `startSignIn` sends the current path as the `callbackURL` when it starts with `/join/` and `/` otherwise ([authentication](authentication.md)). The path is never taken from outside the app.
- **Not guessable, not forever**: a token that isn't found, is expired, was revoked or isn't for the caller answers the same `410`, so a token can't be told from a missing one. Looking up handles and previewing tokens are the two places a stranger could probe; a rate limit on both (Cloudflare's own rules, free) is to be considered at the end of the work, and is noted in `TODO.md`.

## Screen

- **Settings → Profile** (a section after the language and the theme): a `Field` for the username with the hint "People find you by this. Until you pick one, they find you by your email.", Save and, once there is one, Remove, with the handle in use shown under it ("People find you as ana@example.com"). A taken name or a bad one is an error under the field. Offline the field and its buttons are off with a notice.
- **Invite**, in the edit screen of a friend whose other side is a dummy: two ghost buttons, "Copy invitation link" (or the share sheet of the phone where there is one, `navigator.share`) and "Invite by username or email". The second asks for a handle and shows who it is ("Ask Ana Pérez to be your friend?") before sending it. Under them the open invitation is listed ("Open link, until 15 Nov" with Copy link and Revoke, or "Asked Ana Pérez" with Revoke). The form of Add a friend has the same two choices.
- **`/join/$token`** (depth 1): a sheet that says "<Name> wants to be your friend", the name you'll have in it (a `Field`, filled with the name the dummy had) and Accept and Decline. Accepting opens the friend. An invitation that is gone says so in a `Notice` with a link to Groups. The requests made for the caller's handle also appear in Groups ([groups](groups.md)).
