# Groups and friends

The decision is in [ADR-0012](../adr/0012-shared-expenses.md). What the entries hold and how they are split is in [shared expenses](shared-expenses.md), the debts in [balances](balances.md), and how people become friends, are merged and leave the app in [invitations](invitations.md) and [ADR-0014](../adr/0014-people-in-groups.md).

## Rules

- **Kinds.** A `group` has a name and any number of members up to the limit. A `direct` group is a **friend**: two members, no name, no way to add a third. Everything below holds for both unless it says otherwise. A group is made with the app's currency (`DEFAULT_CURRENCY`), stored on it and on every entry.
- **Members.** A member has an `id` (a UUID made on the client, which the entries refer to), a `name`, a `role` (`owner` or `member`) and, once somebody has claimed it, a user. One that nobody has claimed is a **placeholder**, the dummy. The name is the one the group uses for that person: whoever makes a placeholder chooses it, the owner can rename any member, and a user can rename themselves. A user starts with their nickname, else the name of their Google account, as it is when they make or join the group ([invitations](invitations.md)). The email never appears in a group. Two members whose names are the same, ignoring case, are told apart on the screen by a number after the name ("Ana", "Ana (2)", in the order the group lists them); the stored name is untouched. A member whose name is empty is a person who deleted their account, and the screens call them "Deleted user".
- **Friends.** A friend is made by name: a group of two with the maker and a placeholder, so a person without the app is a friend from the start. A placeholder friend becomes a real person when they accept an invitation to be a friend ([invitations](invitations.md)): they take its place and keep everything in the ledger between the two, and the friend is shown by their own name, their nickname or else that of their account, from then on. A real friend is a friend whose other member is a user. A person has at most 100 friends, and can't have two friends that are the same user ([invitations](invitations.md)). Removing a friend is deleting that group, with the same rules as any group.
- **Who is in a group.** A group holds dummies and friends, and nobody else. A member adds a **dummy** by name, or one of their own **friends** who is a real person, and that friend is a member at once: being friends is the consent, and the friend can leave once their balance is zero. When the group has dummies, adding a friend asks whether they **replace** one of them or are a **new** member. Replacing merges the dummy into the friend in the same step (below), so everything already split with the dummy is the friend's. There are no invitations to a group: a new person is invited as a friend first and then added.
- **Making a group.** The maker becomes the `owner` and a member named after their nickname or their Google account, and can add the others at once as dummies by name or from their friends. Making one needs a connection ([offline sync](offline-sync.md)).
- **Who can do what.** Any member adds dummies and their own friends, and adds, edits and deletes entries. Only the owner renames the group, removes other members, merges a placeholder, switches the simplified debts, hands the group over and deletes it. In a friend, either member can do what the owner does, as there is nothing to hand over or to remove. The server decides on every call; the screen only hides what a person can't do.
- **Merging a dummy.** A placeholder can become a real person that is already in the group: it is merged **into a member who is a user**, and only that (two users, or two placeholders, are never merged). It happens when a friend is added in place of a dummy, and the owner can also do it later from the people of the group. The placeholder row stays with `merged_into` set and `left_at` set, and from then on every id of it, in the old entries and in the ones written offline before the merge, means the target, in the balances, the ledger and the lists (`canonicalMember` in `core/`). The server merges only a placeholder, only into a user and never into itself, so an alias is one step; `core` still follows a chain of merges to its end and reads a loop as the member of it that comes first in the group, so a bad row can't make the balances lie. Nothing in the entries is rewritten, so nothing has to be synced again and an offline write never breaks. A merge can't be undone.
- **Leaving and removing.** A member leaves, or the owner removes one, only while that member's balance is zero ([balances](balances.md)); otherwise the answer is `409 unsettled`, and the screen says to settle up first. The member row stays with `left_at` set, so the entries keep naming that person; a placeholder removed the same way. The owner can't leave: they hand the group over to another member who is a user, or delete it. The balance check is not atomic with a write that another device has not synced yet; a late entry can leave a balance on someone who has left, and it shows in the balances as that person's name with "(left)".
- **Deleting a group.** Only the owner, and only when every balance is zero, or when no member but the owner is a user who hasn't left (a group of placeholders hurts nobody). Otherwise `409 unsettled`. The group gets `deleted_at` and is **closed**; nothing else is deleted.
- **Closed groups.** A group is closed for a person when it was deleted, or when they left or were removed. Deleting a group does not delete the expenses: a closed group stays on the person's devices and in their `GET /groups`, read-only. A group that was deleted keeps all its entries readable by its members; one a person left keeps what the group held at the moment they left (`left_seq`), so they never see what the group spends afterwards, and `GET /groups` says `left` to them even if the group is deleted later. Every write to a closed group answers `409 closed`. A closed group does not count toward the limits of 30 open groups and 100 friends, can't be settled or edited, and its balances stay as they were.
- **Archiving.** A person can **archive** a closed group, which only tucks it away: it leaves the list of closed groups for the one of archived groups and can be brought back. It changes nothing else, and in particular not whether the group's expenses are in their own expenses (the next point). An open group can't be archived (`409 not-closed`). Like the next switch it belongs to the person, is kept on the server (`archived_at` on the member) and needs a connection.
- **Show in my expenses.** Each member has a switch per group, on by default, `show_in_expenses`. While it is on, the person's parts of that group's expenses are in their own expenses: in the list, the summary, the charts and the budgets ([when an expense counts](expense-counting.md)). While it is off none of them is, and the group is still there to open. It holds for open, closed and archived groups alike. It belongs to the person and is kept on the server, so every device of the person adds up the same; changing it needs a connection.
- **Limits**: 30 open groups per user (a friend who is in 30 can't be added to another), 100 friends, 30 members per group (those who haven't left), a name of 1 to 40 characters, trimmed, with at least one that shows (the check of the nickname, [invitations](invitations.md)), for a group and for the name a group gives a person (`core/shared-group.ts`, shared by the app and the server).
- **API.** All of it needs a session ([authentication](authentication.md)); a group the caller is not in, and has never been in, answers `404`, so its existence isn't told. Every write answers the group as `GET /groups` lists it for the caller, and a write whose effect is there already answers the group as it is, so sending it again is safe. What only the owner does answers `403 not-owner` to anybody else.
    - `GET /groups` answers the groups and the friends the caller is in, closed ones too: `id`, `kind`, `name`, `currency`, `simplifyDebts`, `closed` (`null`, `deleted` or `left`), `archived`, `me` (the caller's member id), `showInExpenses` and `members` (`id`, `name`, `role`, `claimed`, `leftAt`, `mergedInto`, the left ones included so old entries can name them), never a user id.
    - `PUT /groups/:id` is idempotent. When the group doesn't exist it makes it, with `{ kind, name, currency, me: { id }, others: [{ id, name }] }` (a friend has no name and one other, a placeholder; the server names the maker); when it does, only the owner, and only `{ name, simplifyDebts }` change. An id that belongs to a group the caller is not in, or a member id in use, is `409 id-taken`; over the limits, `409 too-many-groups` or `409 too-many-friends`.
    - `DELETE /groups/:id` deletes it as above (`409 unsettled`) and answers it closed.
    - `PUT /groups/:groupId/members/:memberId` with `{ name }` makes a placeholder (`409 too-many-members` over the limit, `409 id-taken` for the id of a member of another group) or renames a member (the owner anybody, a user themselves). With `{ friend, replaces? }` it adds the real friend whose group is `friend`, named after their nickname or else their account: the caller must be in that friend and it must be open (`422 unknown-friend` otherwise), and the other member must be a user (`409 not-a-user`) who has no member in this group, now or from before (`409 already-member`: a user who left can't be added back) and isn't in 30 open groups (`409 too-many-groups`). The friend goes in as a new member, or in place of the placeholder `replaces` (`422 unknown-member`, `422 member-left`, and `409 not-placeholder` for a member who is or was a user), which is merged into them in the same batch. A friend takes no third member (`409 direct`). `DELETE` on it leaves or removes, setting `left_at` and `left_seq` (`409 unsettled`; `409 owner` when the owner would leave; `409 direct` in a friend, which is deleted instead; `404 unknown-member` for a member the group doesn't have). `POST /groups/:groupId/members/:memberId/merge` with `{ into }` merges. `POST /groups/:groupId/owner` with `{ memberId }` hands the group over.
    - `PUT /groups/:groupId/me` with `{ showInExpenses?, archived? }` sets the switches of the caller.
    - The entries and the invitations have their routes in [shared expenses](shared-expenses.md) and [invitations](invitations.md).

## Screen

- **Groups** (`/groups`, depth 1, a tab of the bottom bar and a word of the header: [bottom navigation](bottom-navigation.md)). From the top: the totals across your open groups and friends, as `Notice`s in words, "You are owed €35.00" and "You owe €12.50" (each only when it isn't zero; "All settled" when both are zero); two `block` buttons, New group and Add a friend, each opening its form under it as a `Disclosure`; then the groups on a `Sheet` labeled "Groups", and the friends on another labeled "Friends". Each row is the name, the balance in words ("You owe €12.50", "Ana owes you €8.00", "Settled") and a chevron, and leads to the group. Rows are `--size-2` apart as in the category list. Without any group, a quiet `Notice` says so over the two buttons. At the end, a `Disclosure` closed by default, "Closed groups", lists the closed ones on a `Sheet` with the word "Deleted" or "You left" and whether each shows in your expenses, and under it, when there are any, a second `Disclosure`, "Archived", with the archived ones; both are left out when empty.
- The form of **New group** has the name and, under it, how to add people: a field to add a dummy by name, one at a time, and a `Select` of your friends who are real people; each listed with a remove button. The main button makes the group and opens it. The form of **Add a friend** has the name of the person; it makes the friend, with that name, and opens it, where the invitation can be sent.
- **A group** (`/groups/$groupId`, depth 2): the title is the name (the other person's name for a friend); then the balance in words, the balances and the settle up form ([balances](balances.md)), the add form, collapsed under a heading like the one at home, and the ledger of entries ([shared expenses](shared-expenses.md)). A ghost link at the end, "Edit group" ("Edit friend"), leads to its settings. A closed group has a quiet `Notice` instead of the forms ("This group was deleted on 12 Nov. Its expenses stay as they were."), the balances and the ledger without any action, and only the link to its settings.
- **Edit a group** (`/groups/$groupId/edit`, depth 3): sections `--size-8` apart.
    - **Show in my expenses**, a `Checkbox` for everybody, with the hint "When it's off, this group's expenses are not in your list, summary, charts or budgets." For a closed group, **Archive** goes with it, a ghost `Button` that puts the group among the archived and, once archived, "Bring back". These are the only sections of a closed group.
    - **Name**, for the owner, and the switch of the simplified debts, a `Checkbox` with a hint ([balances](balances.md)).
    - **People**: a `Sheet` with a row for each member, name first, "You" for the caller, "Owner" for the owner and "Not joined" for a placeholder, with the balance in words. A row opens (a `Disclosure` like the expenses) with its actions as ghost buttons `--size-4` apart: Rename; Merge into… (a placeholder, for the owner, when the group has a member who is a user), which asks in the panel with a `Select` of those members and says it can't be undone; Make owner (a user, for the owner); Remove (for the owner, not on themselves), which asks in the panel as the categories do. Under the sheet, **Add a person**: a field and a button to add a dummy by name, and a `Select` of your friends who are real people and aren't in the group. When the group has dummies, choosing a friend shows a `ChoiceGroup` under it, "Add as a new member" and "In place of <dummy>" for each dummy; the first is the start.
    - **Leave** (everybody but the owner) and **Delete** (the owner), each a `danger` `Button` that asks in a panel: the question, what happens ("The group closes. The expenses stay in your list, and you can turn them off here."), and Confirm and Cancel as ghost buttons, with the focus on Cancel. When a balance isn't zero the panel says so instead and offers the link to settle up.
- **Edit a friend** (the same route): the same sections, except the name, the simplified debts and the people, and with **Invite** when the other side is a dummy ([invitations](invitations.md)).
- **Offline.** The lists and the groups open from the local copy and so do the entries, with the add form. Everything that shapes a group (the forms, the buttons of the panels, the switches, including Show in my expenses and Archive) is off and a `Notice` at the top says why, as in the categories ([offline sync](offline-sync.md)).
- A group the device has never held that is gone answers with a `Notice` and a link back to Groups.
