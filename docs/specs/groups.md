# Groups and friends

The decision is in [ADR-0012](../adr/0012-shared-expenses.md). What the entries hold and how they are split is in [shared expenses](shared-expenses.md), the debts in [balances](balances.md), and how people become friends, are merged and leave the app in [invitations](invitations.md) and [ADR-0014](../adr/0014-people-in-groups.md).

## Rules

- **Kinds.** A `group` has a name and any number of members up to the limit. A `direct` group is a **friend**: two members, no name, no way to add a third, and made only by an accepted invitation, never by a request to make a group. Everything below holds for both unless it says otherwise. A group is made with the app's currency (`DEFAULT_CURRENCY`), stored on it and on every entry; any other currency is `400`, so what the lists and the totals add up is always in one.
- **Members.** A member has an `id` (a UUID made on the client, which the entries refer to), a `name`, a `role` (`owner` or `member`) and, once somebody has claimed it, a user. One that nobody has claimed is a **placeholder**, the dummy. The name is the one the group uses for that person: whoever makes a placeholder chooses it, the owner can rename any member, and a user can rename themselves. A user starts with their nickname, else the name of their Google account cut to 40 characters, as it is when they make or join the group ([invitations](invitations.md)), and with "?" (`UNNAMED_PERSON`) when neither is a name a group takes (one with nothing visible in it), so whatever a person starts with they can send back as their name. The email never appears in a group. Two members whose names are the same, ignoring case, are told apart on the screen by a number after the name ("Ana", "Ana (2)", in the order the group lists them); the stored name is untouched. A member whose name is empty is a person who deleted their account, and the screens call them "Deleted user".
- **Friends.** A friend who uses the app is made by an invitation: a link that, once accepted, makes a group of two with who sent it and who accepted it, both users ([invitations](invitations.md)). There are no loose fake friends: nobody becomes a friend by being typed, and a person without the app exists only as a dummy inside a group, added in the People of that group. A friend's other member is a user, shown by their nickname or else the name of their account, for as long as the friend is open (it closes for the person left when the other deletes their account). A person has at most 100 friends, and can't have two friends that are the same user ([invitations](invitations.md)). Removing a friend is deleting that group, with the same rules as any group. A friend also ends, for the other person, when somebody deletes their account: it is closed for the one left, as if it had been deleted, so a friend who is open always has a real person on the other side ([authentication](authentication.md)).
- **Who is in a group.** A group holds dummies and friends, and nobody else. A member adds a **dummy** by name, or one of their own **friends** who is a real person, and that friend is a member at once: being friends is the consent, and the friend can leave once their balance is zero. One who left, or whom the owner removed, is added the same way and comes back (Coming back, below). When the group has dummies, adding a friend asks the owner whether they **replace** one of them or are a **new** member; anybody else adds them as new. Replacing merges the dummy into the friend in the same step (below), so everything already split with the dummy is the friend's. There are no invitations to a group: a new person is invited as a friend first and then added.
- **Making a group.** The maker becomes the `owner` and a member named after their nickname or their Google account, and can add the others at once as dummies by name or from their friends. Making one needs a connection ([offline sync](offline-sync.md)).
- **Who can do what.** Any member adds dummies and their own friends, and adds, edits and deletes entries. Only the owner renames the group, removes other members, merges a placeholder (also by adding a friend in place of it), switches the simplified debts, hands the group over and deletes it. In a friend, either member can do what the owner does, as there is nothing to hand over or to remove. The server decides on every call; the screen only hides what a person can't do.
- **Merging a dummy.** A placeholder can become a real person that is already in the group: it is merged **into a member who is a user**, and only that (two users, or two placeholders, are never merged). It happens when a friend is added in place of a dummy, and the owner can also do it later from the people of the group. The placeholder row stays with `merged_into` set and `left_at` set, and from then on every id of it, in the old entries and in the ones written offline before the merge, means the target, in the balances, the ledger and the lists (`canonicalMember` in `core/`). The server merges only a placeholder, only into a user and never into itself, so an alias is one step; `core` still follows a chain of merges to its end and reads a loop as the member of it that comes first in the group, so a bad row can't make the balances lie. Nothing in the entries is rewritten, so nothing has to be synced again and an offline write never breaks. A merge can't be undone.
- **Leaving and removing.** A member leaves, or the owner removes one, only while that member's balance is zero ([balances](balances.md)); otherwise the answer is `409 unsettled`, and the screen says to settle up first. The member row stays with `left_at` set, so the entries keep naming that person; a placeholder removed the same way. The owner can't leave: they hand the group over to another member who is a user, or delete it. An entry can't then move what that person pays or owes (`422 member-left`, [shared expenses](shared-expenses.md)), but deleting an entry that names them, or taking such a delete back, can leave a balance on them, and so can a write at the very moment they leave, as the balance check isn't atomic with it; it shows in the balances as that person's name with "(left)", until they come back (Coming back, below).
- **Coming back.** A member who left, or whom the owner removed, can be added again, and it makes no difference which of the two it was. A person has one row in a group for good (the unique `(group_id, user_id)`), so the row is **reopened** and no second one is made: `left_at`, `left_seq` and `archived_at` become null, and the row keeps its id, its name (what the group calls them), its role (always `member`, as the owner can't leave), its place in the group and its `show_in_expenses` switch. The entries that named them go on naming them, and their balance, zero when they left, goes on from there; the "(left)" mark goes away and the entries that name them can be edited again. Whoever may add a friend may bring someone back, with the same consent: being friends. The group is open for them again, also if they had archived it, and they read the whole history, also what the group wrote while they were away, and all its people, also those who joined meanwhile; their device pulls what it lacks from its own cursor ([offline sync](offline-sync.md)). They take a place among the people of the group (30) and among their open groups (20, `409 too-many-groups`). They take none among the rows the group ever had (50), as no row is made, nor among their closed groups (20), as the closed group they leave behind is this one. A friend still takes no third member (`409 direct`) and a deleted group stays closed (`409 closed`). A request sent again after an answer that never came answers `409 already-member`, since the friend is in by then: the screen says so and the groups it reads again show them in.
- **Deleting a group.** Only the owner, and only when every balance is zero, or when no member but the owner is a user who hasn't left (a group of placeholders hurts nobody). Otherwise `409 unsettled`. The group gets `deleted_at` and is **closed**; nothing else is deleted. A group also closes, with no owner left, when its owner deletes their account and the others who used the app have left; otherwise it goes to the one who joined first. A friend closes for the other person when one of the two deletes their account. A group where nobody else has an account (a group of placeholders, a friend whose both people deleted their accounts) is not closed but erased, with its name, its people and its entries ([authentication](authentication.md)).
- **Closed groups.** A group is closed for a person when it was deleted, or when they left or were removed, and it stops being closed for them if they come back (Coming back, above). Deleting a group does not delete the expenses: a closed group stays on the person's devices and in their `GET /groups`, read-only. A group that was deleted keeps all its entries readable by its members; one a person left keeps what the group held at the moment they left (`left_seq`), so, for as long as they are out, they never see what the group spends afterwards, nor an entry edited or deleted afterwards: it moves past their cursor, so their device keeps the version it held, and an entry it hadn't pulled never reaches them (which is why leaving pushes and pulls the group first, [offline sync](offline-sync.md)), nor who joins it: their `GET /groups` lists only the members added before they left, as they were then (a later leave or merge isn't shown), though with the names and the roles they have now (the owner they see is the current one), and `GET /groups` says `left` to them even if the group is deleted later. A closed group no longer changes, so a device pulls its entries only until all of them are there, unless the group opens again for the person ([offline sync](offline-sync.md)). Every write to a closed group answers `409 closed`, before the server looks at who or what it names, so that a person who left can't tell by an answer what happened after (a handover, a merge, a removal, the deletion of the group); the one exception is their own leave sent again, which answers the group. A closed group does not count toward the limits of 20 open groups and 100 friends (it counts toward the 20 closed ones, below), can't be settled or edited, and its balances stay as they were.
- **Archiving.** A person can **archive** a closed group, which only tucks it away: it leaves the list of closed groups for the one of archived groups and can be brought back. It changes nothing else, and in particular not whether the group's expenses are in their own expenses (the next point), but for the limit of closed groups, which an archived one no longer counts toward (Limits, below). An open group can't be archived (`409 not-closed`). Like the next switch it belongs to the person, is kept on the server (`archived_at` on the member) and needs a connection.
- **Show in my expenses.** Each member has a switch per group, on by default, `show_in_expenses`. While it is on, the person's parts of that group's expenses are in their own expenses: in the list, the summary, the charts and the budgets ([when an expense counts](expense-counting.md)). While it is off none of them is, and the group is still there to open. It holds for open, closed and archived groups alike. It belongs to the person and is kept on the server, so every device of the person adds up the same; changing it needs a connection.
- **Limits**: 20 open groups per user (a friend who is in 20 can't be added to another), 100 friends, 20 closed groups (`MAX_CLOSED_GROUPS`, every group and friend that is closed for the person and that they haven't archived, deleted or left, which another member can pile onto them by adding them to groups and closing these; **archiving one frees its place**, and bringing it back takes it again; a person who has them can neither make a group or a friend nor be added to one, `409 too-many-closed-groups` to whoever tries, the maker or the one who adds them (but for the group they left, which they can be brought back to: it stops being closed), counted by the `user_id` of their members and just before the write like the others; deleting an account never counts it, so a group handed over to a person who has them is handed over all the same), 30 members per group (those who haven't left, counted in the same statement that adds one, so adds at the same moment can't pass it) and 50 rows of members a group ever has (`MAX_GROUP_MEMBER_ROWS`, those who left and those merged included, since a member's row is never deleted, so removing people never makes room for more; the same statement counts them, and the add answers `409 too-many-members-ever`, which tells the person to make a new group; it is also the most shares an entry can hold), a name of 1 to 40 characters, trimmed, with at least one that shows and none that overrides or isolates the direction of the text (the checks of the nickname, [invitations](invitations.md)), for a group and for the name a group gives a person (`core/shared-group.ts`, shared by the app and the server). The limits of groups and friends are counted just before a group is made, so groups made at the same moment can pass them by a few.
- **API.** All of it needs a session ([authentication](authentication.md)). Every route answers a group the caller is not in, and has never been in, with the same `404` as a group that doesn't exist, so its existence isn't told. The one exception is making a group: an id that is taken answers `409 id-taken`, which tells the caller only that the id they sent is in use. Every write answers the group as `GET /groups` lists it for the caller, and a write whose effect is there already answers the group as it is, so sending it again is safe, also several times at once. What only the owner does answers `403 not-owner` to anybody else. The ids of groups and members, in the path and in a body, are UUIDs read in lower case, so two spellings of one id are the same id.
    - `GET /groups` answers the groups and the friends the caller is in, closed ones too: `id`, `kind`, `name`, `currency`, `simplifyDebts`, `closed` (`null`, `deleted` or `left`), `archived`, `me` (the caller's member id), `showInExpenses` and `members` (`id`, `name`, `role`, `claimed`, `leftAt`, `mergedInto`, the left ones included so old entries can name them), never a user id.
    - `PUT /groups/:id` is idempotent. When the group doesn't exist it makes it, with `{ kind: 'group', name, currency, me: { id }, others: [{ id, name }] }` (the others are placeholders; the server names the maker, and the group lists the maker first and the others in the order sent); `kind: 'direct'` is a `400` whether the group exists or not, as a friend is made only by accepting an invitation ([invitations](invitations.md)). When it does exist, only the owner, and only `{ name, simplifyDebts }` change. An id that belongs to a group the caller is not in, or a member id in use, is `409 id-taken`; over the limits, `409 too-many-groups` or `409 too-many-closed-groups`.
    - `DELETE /groups/:id` deletes it as above (`409 unsettled`) and answers it closed.
    - `PUT /groups/:groupId/members/:memberId` with `{ name }` makes a placeholder (`409 too-many-members` over the limit of people in it, `409 too-many-members-ever` over the rows it ever had, `409 id-taken` for the id of a member of another group) or renames a member (the owner anybody, a user themselves). With `{ friend, replaces? }` it adds the real friend whose group is `friend`, named after their nickname or else their account: the caller must be in that friend and it must be open (`422 unknown-friend` otherwise), and the person on the other side, who is always a user, must not be in this group (`409 already-member`, for one who hasn't left), must not be in 20 open groups (`409 too-many-groups`) and, unless they are coming back, must not have 20 closed ones (`409 too-many-closed-groups`). The friend goes in as a new member, or, for the owner (`403 not-owner`), in place of the placeholder `replaces` (`422 unknown-member`, `422 member-left`, and `409 not-placeholder` for a member who is or was a user), which is merged into them in the same batch. A friend who has a row here that left, whether they left or were removed, is not added as a new member: that row is reopened (Coming back, above), the `memberId` of the path is not used, and a `replaces` merges its dummy into that row in the same batch, only if the row was reopened. The reopen is one statement that checks its own conditions (a user who left and wasn't merged, the group not deleted, room for one more person, counted without the dummy it replaces), so two requests at the same moment reopen it once and the other answers `409 already-member` and writes nothing; `409 too-many-members` when the group has no room, and `409 closed` when it was deleted since the group was read. A friend takes no third member (`409 direct`). `DELETE` on it leaves or removes, setting `left_at` and `left_seq` (`409 unsettled`; `409 owner` when the owner would leave; `409 direct` in a friend, which is deleted instead; `404 unknown-member` for a member the group doesn't have). `POST /groups/:groupId/members/:memberId/merge` with `{ into }` merges, and refuses a member who is or was a user (`409 not-placeholder`), a merge into itself (`409 into-itself`), into a member who isn't a user (`409 not-a-user`), a placeholder that has left and a target that has left (`409` and `422 member-left`), and a friend (`409 direct`). `POST /groups/:groupId/owner` with `{ memberId }` hands the group over, only to a member who is a user (`409 not-a-user`) and hasn't left (`422 member-left`), and not in a friend (`409 direct`). Both are guarded in the SQL that writes them, not only in what the route read: the promotion runs only while the target hasn't left (`422 member-left`, also when their leave came at the same moment) and a leave is written only for a member who isn't the owner (`409 owner`, also when the handover came at the same moment), so the two at the same moment never leave an owner who has left.
    - `PUT /groups/:groupId/me` with `{ showInExpenses?, archived? }` sets the switches of the caller, also in a group closed for them; archiving an open one is `409 not-closed`.
    - The entries and the invitations have their routes in [shared expenses](shared-expenses.md) and [invitations](invitations.md).

## Screen

- **Groups** (`/groups`, depth 1, a tab of the bottom bar and a word of the header: [bottom navigation](bottom-navigation.md)). From the top: the totals across your open groups and friends, as `Notice`s in words, "You are owed €35.00" and "You owe €12.50" (each only when it isn't zero; "All settled" when both are zero); then two sections, `--size-6` apart, each under a small `h2` and always there, empty or not. **Groups** holds New group, a `block` button that opens its form under it as a `Disclosure`, and the open groups. **Friends** holds Invite a friend, a primary `block` button that makes a link at once, with a hint under it and, when there are any, the open links of the person on a `Sheet` labeled "Invitation links" (an `h3`) ([invitations](invitations.md)), and the open friends; with none, a quiet `Notice` says that a friend appears there when somebody accepts the link, unless there is no open group either, when the `Notice` over the sections says it. Nobody can be added to the friends by name: a friend is somebody who accepted a link. The controls and the headings are there also while the groups can't be loaded, which a `Notice` says, and the line about the friends waits until they are read. In a section, control and list are `--size-4` apart, and the list is a column of cards, each a link whose whole box is the touch target. Each row is the name, the balance in words ("You owe €12.50", "Ana owes you €8.00", "Settled") and a chevron, and leads to the group. A group whose expenses haven't reached the device yet shows no balance, and the totals wait until every open group has them, so they never leave one out; when one of them can't be loaded, a `Notice` says so in their place. Rows are `--size-2` apart as in the category list. Without any group or friend, a quiet `Notice` says so over the sections, and that a friend is invited with a link. At the end, a `Disclosure` closed by default, "Closed groups", lists the closed ones on a `Sheet` with the word "Deleted" (for a friend, "No longer a friend") or "You left" and whether each shows in your expenses ("In your expenses", "Not in your expenses"), and under it, when there are any, a second `Disclosure`, "Archived", with the archived ones; both are left out when empty.
- The form of **New group** has the name and, under it, how to add people: a field to add a dummy by name, one at a time, and a `Select` of your open friends (left out when there are none) with Add friend beside it; each listed with a remove button; Enter in the field adds the person, and a name still in it when the group is made goes in with the rest. A group takes at most 30 people, the maker included, and the field says so when there is no room left. The main button makes the group with its dummies, then adds the friends one by one, and opens it. Every person listed keeps the member id they will have while the screen is open, so pressing it again after a failure finishes the same group; and since a make sent again answers the group made first, the calls after it put that group right against the list as it is now, adding, renaming or removing whoever changed. A friend refused there is said in words, a limit of groups as the friend's.
- **A group** (`/groups/$groupId`, depth 2): the title is the name (the other person's name for a friend), and under it the writes to the group that the server refused, each with its Dismiss ([shared expenses](shared-expenses.md)); then the balance in words, the balances and the settle up button ([balances](balances.md)) and the ledger of entries ([shared expenses](shared-expenses.md)). The screen has no add form of its own: the "+" adds an expense from any screen and, while this group is open, starts it here ([shared expenses](shared-expenses.md)); Edit and Duplicate of the ledger open the same sheet. A ghost link at the end, "Edit group" ("Edit friend"), leads to its settings. A closed group has a quiet `Notice` instead of the settle up button, which says why with no day, as `GET /groups` doesn't carry it ("This group was deleted. Its expenses stay as they were.", for a friend "This person is no longer your friend. What you shared stays as it was.", and "You left this group. It stays as it was the day you left."), the balances and the ledger without any action, and only the link to its settings.
- **Edit a group** (`/groups/$groupId/edit`, depth 3): sections `--size-8` apart.
    - **Show in my expenses**, a `Checkbox` for everybody, with the hint "When it's off, this group's expenses are not in your list, summary, charts or budgets." For a closed group, **Archive** goes with it, a ghost `Button` that puts the group among the archived and, once archived, "Bring back". These are the only sections of a closed group.
    - **Name**, for the owner, and under it **Simplify debts**, a `Checkbox` with a hint ([balances](balances.md)), off to begin with. Only the owner is shown it (a friend has none, and a closed group does not offer it), it needs a connection like the rest, and it shows what was asked for until the server answers or refuses in words.
    - **People**: a `Sheet` with a row for each member who hasn't left (who left shows in the balances), name first, "You" for the caller, "Owner" for the owner and "Not joined" for a placeholder, with the balance in words. A row opens (a `Disclosure` like the expenses) with its actions as ghost buttons `--size-4` apart: Rename; Merge into… (a placeholder, for the owner), which asks in the panel with a `Select` of the members who are users (you as "Ana (You)", Ana being the name the group gives you), the focus on it and nobody chosen (Merge is off until somebody is), and says it can't be undone; Make owner (another user, for the owner), which asks in the panel with the focus on Cancel; Remove (for the owner, not on themselves), which asks in the panel as the categories do, or, when that person's balance on the device isn't zero, says to settle up first instead; the server still decides, and its refusals are said in words. Once the person is removed or merged, or made owner (their row then has nothing to open), the focus goes to the label of the sheet. A row with nothing the person may do doesn't open. Under the sheet, **Add a person**: a field and a button to add a dummy by name, and a `Select` of your open friends, with Add friend under it. A member carries no user, so the device can't leave out a friend who is in the group already: the server's `409 already-member` says so in words, "That friend is in the group already.". A friend who left is offered like the rest, and adding them brings them back (Coming back, above). When the group has dummies, choosing a friend shows the owner a `ChoiceGroup` under it, "Add as a new member" and "In place of <dummy>" for each dummy; the first is the start.
    - **Leave** (everybody but the owner) and **Delete** (the owner), each a `block` `danger` `Button` at the end that asks in a panel taking its place: the question, what happens ("The group closes for everybody in it. The expenses stay in your list, and you can turn them off here."; for Leave, "The group closes for you as it is today", and that a friend of theirs in the group can add them back, when they would see everything written in the meantime), and Confirm and Cancel as ghost buttons, with the focus on Cancel, which gives it back to the button. When a balance isn't zero (yours to leave; any, to delete, unless nobody else who uses the app is still in the group) the panel says so instead and offers the link to settle up, and so does the server's `409 unsettled`; a ledger the device never pulled asks, and the server decides. Once the group is closed the screen goes to Groups, where it is among the closed ones; any other refusal is said under it in words.
- **Edit a friend** (the same route): the same sections, except the name, the simplified debts and the people, with Delete as **Remove friend** for either side.
- **Offline.** The lists and the groups open from the local copy and so do the entries and adding one. Everything that shapes a group (the forms, the buttons of the panels, the switches, including Show in my expenses and Archive) is off and a `Notice` at the top says why, as in the categories ([offline sync](offline-sync.md)).
- A group the device has never held that is gone answers with a `Notice` and a link back to Groups, and so does any group while the device has never heard from the server.
