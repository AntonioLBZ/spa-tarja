# Charts

## Rules

The charts need no endpoint: they aggregate the expenses the device already holds (`core/reports/charts.ts`), so they work offline and see unsynced changes. Weeks start on the first day of the week of the browser's language, Monday when it can't tell ([ADR-0039](../adr/0039-the-week-starts-where-the-browser-says.md)), a range includes both of its ends, and every row of a chart is a day, a week or a month of that range, empty ones too, up to 400 rows. The total of a range is compared with the same span of the calendar right before it (`comparison`, [ADR-0043](../adr/0043-the-charts-pick-a-category-from-the-split-and-list-what-they-count.md)), whatever the grouping. A range of whole months (from a 1st to the last day of a month) compares with as many calendar months right before it (`previousRange`): October with September, twelve months with the twelve before. Any other range compares with as many days right before it, so twelve whole weeks with the twelve weeks before and 15 to 31 October with the 17 days before. A range still running is compared only up to today, and the earlier span up to the same point: the same day of the month for months, or the last day of a shorter month, and as many days in for any other range. On 25 November the month compares 1 to 25 November with 1 to 25 October, on 30 March March so far with the whole of February, and twelve months running to 25 November 2026 with the twelve before up to 25 November 2025, while the stat row still shows the total of the whole range. A range that starts after today compares with nothing. The owner asked for this rule on 2026-11-25, replacing that of 2026-10-29 (as many days right before, whatever the range). Both sides go through `spendingTotal` with the same category. The scale of a chart is worked out there too (`niceScale`: 3 to 5 round ticks from 0).

## Screen

- `/charts`, one level deeper than the summary it is reached from. Everything is computed on the device from the local expenses, so it works offline.
- Filters on top: group by (day, week or month, a choice group) and from/to dates. The category is picked from the split (below), not among the filters. Until the person picks dates the range follows the grouping: the whole current month for days, twelve weeks counting this one for weeks, twelve months counting this one for months, each up to the end of its period so that what is still to be charged in it shows, as it does in the summary. Picking dates keeps them whatever the grouping, and a button brings the defaults back. Only days between 2000 and 2099 count, because a date field passes through years like 0002 and 0020 while one is typed; whatever doesn't make a day yet stays in the field without touching the chart, and leaving the field puts the range back. A start after the end, or a range of more than 400 rows, is refused with a message tied to the fields instead of an empty or endless chart; for a range that is too long the total and the split by category still show. Weeks start where the browser's week does (ADR-0039).
- **The stat row** comes first under the filters, as a `Card`: the total of the whole range in the category picked, in bold, after "Total", or "Total in Food" while a category is picked, since what picks it is at the foot of the screen, and under it the change against the same span of the calendar before it (see Rules), in the same category: an arrow (↑ or ↓, none when it rounds to 0%), the signed share and what is compared. When the whole range is compared, the line names the earlier span, as in "↑ +12% vs. Sep 2026". While the range is still running only its days up to today are compared, so the line names both sides, as in "↓ -50%, Oct 1 – 3 vs. Sep 1 – 3". A span of whole months reads as months ("Sep 2026"), any other as days, with the year when it isn't this one. When nothing was spent in the earlier span, the line says so instead of a share; a range that starts after today has no line at all. The arrow and the sign carry the direction; no color does, and spending more is not an error, so the danger color stays out of it. A range too long to draw still has its stat row, its split and its expenses.
- **The spending chart** has a column for each day, week or month of the range, oldest first, empty ones included, in one `<svg>` that stretches to the width of the screen's column (a `viewBox` with `preserveAspectRatio="none"`) at a fixed height. Columns are flat rectangles in the accent (`--color-bg-accent`) with square corners, a quarter of their slot apart while there are 60 or fewer; past that they touch and read as a silhouette. A column with anything spent is never flatter than 2 px, so a small amount never reads as nothing. A column is never narrower than 1 px, so 400 days on a phone scroll sideways inside the chart's box and the page never does.
- The scale is round (`niceScale`): 3 to 5 ticks, 1, 2, 2.5 or 5 times a power of ten apart, from 0 to the first one that holds the biggest column (one euro when nothing was spent). Every tick but 0 is a hairline (`--color-border-subtle`) across the plot, and its amount is an HTML label on the left at `--font-size-s`, so it follows the font tokens; a scale in whole euros leaves out the cents. The plot box has the notch: its thick ink left edge is the axis and its bottom edge the baseline. Under it, the first and the last row are named in short, also in HTML.
- Two columns are marked by shape, not by color. The biggest one wears an ink cap, a short bar as wide as the column just above its top (the first of them on a tie, none when nothing was spent). Today's row is framed by an ink hairline from the top of the plot down to the baseline, so it shows even when nothing was spent that day.
- **Reading a column.** The chart is one tab stop. The arrows move the selection one row (from none, → picks the first and ← the last), Home and End jump to the first and the last (with Alt, Ctrl or ⌘ held they are left to the browser, whose Alt+← goes back), and a tap or a click picks the column under it: the whole slot, as tall as the plot, answers, so a thin column needn't be hit. Dragging picks nothing, so a swipe still scrolls a chart wider than its box. The selected slot sits on `--color-bg-secondary` inside an ink frame of 2 px (the frame of today is 1 px, so the two don't read as one), and its column turns ink, also never flatter than 2 px, so a picked empty row still shows on the baseline. The frame is a mark of shape, not of color, so it still tells the column apart where the accent is close to ink, as gray is in dark (1.5 to 1). On the first and last column the frame loses 1 px on the outer side, which the plot box clips; with hundreds of columns it reads as a vertical line. A line under the chart (`aria-live="polite"`) reads the selection: the row's name and its amount, with the category when one is picked ("Sun, Oct 25: €48.99", "Sun, Oct 25, Food: €3.00"); before any, it says how to pick one. The selection stays on its row when the category changes and goes away when the row leaves the range or the grouping changes. The focus ring goes around the plot box. The chart is not a slider, and not an image either, since an image has no keys: screen readers in browse mode, such as NVDA and JAWS, keep the arrows for themselves, so their users read the summary label and the table instead.
- **The text alternative.** The `<svg>` is a `role="group"` with `aria-roledescription` "chart" ("gráfico"), since it takes the focus and the arrow keys, and a label made from the numbers: how many rows, the total and the biggest row ("Spending over 31 days: €89.49 in total. Highest: €48.99 (Sun, Oct 25)."). Under the readout, a closed `Disclosure`, "Show the numbers", holds a table with a row for each day, week or month, empty ones included: its name (with the year when the range goes over a New Year) and its amount.
- **The split by category** comes after the chart: one strip, `--size-4` tall, cut into a segment per category, the biggest first from the start, each as wide as its share, in the color of its category (the same as its icon, gray for "Other" and for a deleted category) and parted by a 2 px gap of the page color. It is `aria-hidden`: the rows under it carry the numbers. The rows are `Card`s in a `CardList`, each with the icon and the name (the color alone says nothing), the amount and the share, and no bar of its own; a quiet hint between the strip and the rows says a row can be pressed. **Each row picks its category** (ADR-0043): it is one button as wide as the row (`aria-pressed`, named by what it shows), and pressing it narrows the chart, the stat row and the expenses below to that category, pressing it again lets go of it, one category at a time; the pick is the screen's state and is lost on leaving it. Since the numbers it changes are above it, out of sight on a phone, the new total is said through the `Announcer` ("Food: €15.50 in total", "Every category: €89.49 in total"). The total line of the stat row can take the focus (`tabIndex=-1`). The picked row is told by shape: its name in bold and a `check` at its end, in a column every row keeps so nothing moves. The split itself ignores the pick, so it still answers where the money went; categories that took the same go in the order of their names. A picked category that took nothing in a new range stays as the last row, at zero and with no segment, so it can be let go of, also when nothing at all was spent and the quiet `Notice` says so; letting go of it moves the focus to the first row that stays, or to the total when none does, since the row that had it goes away. Until the categories are known, an expense is never moved to the fallback. A category that was deleted shows as the fallback category, like in the list. With nothing spent, a quiet `Notice` says so instead.
- **The expenses** come last (ADR-0043), under a heading "Expenses": a ticket ([the ticket](../../design-system/DESIGN.md#3-shape)) for each row of the chart that has anything, newest first, labelled with the row's name as the table names it, and on it what counts on that row, by the day it counts on and then the newest made first, at what it counts (`countedExpenses`, the same `countedOn` and `countedCents` as the columns, so a ticket's foot "Total" is its column), in the category picked or all of them; an expense that counts nothing (a restaurant card one whose part is zero) is left out. The rows look like those of the expense list and open nothing: no panel, no Edit, Duplicate or Delete, which stay in the list. A part of a plan is a row of its own that says which part it is ("Payment 3 of 12"), an expense counted on another day than it was made (a credit card, or any row under a week or a month) says the day it was made, and a shared row is your part and names its group and who paid; a settlement is never there. A hundred rows are drawn and "Show more" adds a hundred, its focus going to the first row it brings in; a change of range, grouping or category starts over. With nothing counted, or dates refused, there is no section; a range too long to draw keeps it, as it keeps its split.
- Category colors paint the icon and the segments of the split (see [Category colors](../../design-system/DESIGN.md#category-colors)); the columns of the spending chart stay in the accent. The color never says which segment is which alone: the rows under the strip give each one its icon and its name.

The columns, the total, the comparison, the split by category and the expenses include your part of the shared expenses, counted as any other expense is (by `countedOn`: a credit card of yours counts on the day it is charged); a settlement is not in them ([when an expense counts](expense-counting.md)).
