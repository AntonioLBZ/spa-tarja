# Charts

## Rules

The charts need no endpoint: they aggregate the expenses the device already holds (`core/charts.ts`), so they work offline and see unsynced changes. Weeks start on Monday, a range includes both of its ends, and every row of a chart is a day, a week or a month of that range, empty ones too, up to 400 rows. The total of a range is compared with an earlier span as long as the one compared (`comparison`). A range of whole rows that has already ended compares with as many rows right before it (`previousRange`): days with as many days, twelve weeks from Monday with the twelve before, and October by month with September once October is over. Any other range that has ended compares with as many days right before it, so 15 to 31 October with the 17 days before. A range still running is compared only up to today, its days so far against as many days right before its start: on 3 October the month by day compares 1 to 3 October with 28 to 30 September, while the stat row still shows the total of the whole month. A range that starts after today compares with nothing. The owner confirmed this rule on 2026-10-29. Both sides go through `spendingTotal` with the same category. The scale of a chart is worked out there too (`niceScale`: 3 to 5 round ticks from 0).

## Screen

- `/charts`, one level deeper than the summary it is reached from. Everything is computed on the device from the local expenses, so it works offline.
- Filters on top: group by (day, week or month, a choice group), category (a select that starts at all of them) and from/to dates. Until the person picks dates the range follows the grouping: the whole current month for days, twelve weeks counting this one for weeks, twelve months counting this one for months, each up to the end of its period so that what is still to be charged in it shows, as it does in the summary. Picking dates keeps them whatever the grouping, and a button brings the defaults back. Only days between 2000 and 2099 count, because a date field passes through years like 0002 and 0020 while one is typed; whatever doesn't make a day yet stays in the field without touching the chart, and leaving the field puts the range back. A start after the end, or a range of more than 400 rows, is refused with a message tied to the fields instead of an empty or endless chart; for a range that is too long the total and the split by category still show. Weeks start on Monday.
- **The stat row** comes first under the filters, as a `Card`: the total of the whole range in the category picked, in bold, and under it the change against an earlier span of the same length (see Rules), in the same category: an arrow (↑ or ↓, none when it rounds to 0%), the signed share and what is compared. When the whole range is compared, the line names the earlier span, as in "↑ +12% vs. Aug 31 – Sep 30". While the range is still running only its days up to today are compared, so the line names both sides, as in "↓ -50%, Oct 1 – 3 vs. Sep 28 – 30". A span of whole months reads as months ("Sep 2026"), any other as days, with the year when it isn't this one. When nothing was spent in the earlier span, the line says so instead of a share; a range that starts after today has no line at all. The arrow and the sign carry the direction; no color does, and spending more is not an error, so the danger color stays out of it. A range too long to draw still has its stat row and its split.
- **The spending chart** has a column for each day, week or month of the range, oldest first, empty ones included, in one `<svg>` that stretches to the width of the screen's column (a `viewBox` with `preserveAspectRatio="none"`) at a fixed height. Columns are flat rectangles in the accent (`--color-bg-accent`) with square corners, a quarter of their slot apart while there are 60 or fewer; past that they touch and read as a silhouette. A column with anything spent is never flatter than 2 px, so a small amount never reads as nothing. A column is never narrower than 1 px, so 400 days on a phone scroll sideways inside the chart's box and the page never does.
- The scale is round (`niceScale`): 3 to 5 ticks, 1, 2, 2.5 or 5 times a power of ten apart, from 0 to the first one that holds the biggest column (one euro when nothing was spent). Every tick but 0 is a hairline (`--color-border-subtle`) across the plot, and its amount is an HTML label on the left at `--font-size-s`, so it follows the font tokens; a scale in whole euros leaves out the cents. The plot box has the notch: its thick ink left edge is the axis and its bottom edge the baseline. Under it, the first and the last row are named in short, also in HTML.
- Two columns are marked by shape, not by color. The biggest one wears an ink cap, a short bar as wide as the column just above its top (the first of them on a tie, none when nothing was spent). Today's row is framed by an ink hairline from the top of the plot down to the baseline, so it shows even when nothing was spent that day.
- **Reading a column.** The chart is one tab stop. The arrows move the selection one row (from none, → picks the first and ← the last), Home and End jump to the first and the last (with Alt, Ctrl or ⌘ held they are left to the browser, whose Alt+← goes back), and a tap or a click picks the column under it: the whole slot, as tall as the plot, answers, so a thin column needn't be hit. Dragging picks nothing, so a swipe still scrolls a chart wider than its box. The selected slot sits on `--color-bg-secondary` inside an ink frame of 2 px (the frame of today is 1 px, so the two don't read as one), and its column turns ink, also never flatter than 2 px, so a picked empty row still shows on the baseline. The frame is a mark of shape, not of color, so it still tells the column apart where the accent is close to ink, as gray is in dark (1.5 to 1). On the first and last column the frame loses 1 px on the outer side, which the plot box clips; with hundreds of columns it reads as a vertical line. A line under the chart (`aria-live="polite"`) reads the selection: the row's name and its amount, with the category when one is picked ("Sun, Oct 25: €48.99", "Sun, Oct 25, Food: €3.00"); before any, it says how to pick one. The selection stays on its row when the category changes and goes away when the row leaves the range or the grouping changes. The focus ring goes around the plot box. The chart stays an image, not a slider: screen readers in browse mode, such as NVDA and JAWS, keep the arrows for themselves, so their users read the summary label and the table instead.
- **The text alternative.** The `<svg>` has `role="img"` and a label made from the numbers: how many rows, the total and the biggest row ("Spending over 31 days: €89.49 in total. Highest: €48.99 (Sun, Oct 25)."). Under the readout, a closed `Disclosure`, "Show the numbers", holds a table with a row for each day, week or month, empty ones included: its name (with the year when the range goes over a New Year) and its amount.
- **The split by category** comes last: one strip, `--size-4` tall, cut into a segment per category, the biggest first from the start, each as wide as its share, in the color of its category (the same as its icon, gray for "Other" and for a deleted category) and parted by a 2 px gap of the page color. It is `aria-hidden`: the rows under it carry the numbers. The rows are `Card`s in a `CardList`, each with the icon and the name (the color alone says nothing), the amount and the share, and no bar of its own. The split ignores the category filter, so it still answers where the money went; categories that took the same go in the order of their names. Until the categories are known, an expense is never moved to the fallback. A category that was deleted shows as the fallback category, like in the list. With nothing spent, a quiet `Notice` says so instead.
- Category colors paint the icon and the segments of the split (see [Category colors](../../design-system/DESIGN.md#category-colors)); the columns of the spending chart stay in the accent. The color never says which segment is which alone: the rows under the strip give each one its icon and its name.
