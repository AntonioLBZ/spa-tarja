# Tarja — Design

Source of truth for the visual and interaction rules of the design system. `src/assets/styles/tokens.css` implements the tokens; if a rule changes, update this document first. How each screen behaves is in [`docs/specs/`](../docs/specs/) and the decisions are in [`docs/adr/`](../docs/adr/).

## 1. Principles

- **Simple for the user.** Few things on screen, few choices, sensible defaults. Logging an expense should take seconds and work one-handed.
- **Minimalist.** Black and white do the work. Color is rare and always means something: the accent marks what can be acted on, and a category color marks a category.
- **Flat.** Solid fills only.
- **Sharp.** No rounded corners.
- **Mobile-first.** The phone layout is the base; larger screens are an extension.
- **Motion explains.** Transitions show what changed. They never decorate.

## 2. Color and themes

### Flat colors

Solid colors only: no gradients, no blur. Separation comes from borders and from the two surface tones, not from elevation. The one exception is the soft shadow (`--shadow-popup`) around a list that opens over the page, such as the options of a `Select`: it tells the list from what lies under it. The same shadow, with the same color, is cast by the bottom bar of a phone, the sticky header and the actions stuck to the foot of a sheet onto the content scrolled under them, and only while there is some ([ADR-0018](../docs/adr/0018-a-shadow-where-content-scrolls-under-an-edge.md)), and by the `Banner`, which floats over the page like the list (chosen by the owner in Storybook, 2026-11-14). Nothing else casts a shadow.

### Palette

The base is white (light theme) or black (dark theme), with text in the opposite one. The dark theme is true black. Everything else is a neutral gray, one accent (picked from a fixed list), one danger color and the category palette.

| Token                       | Light       | Dark        | Use                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------------------- | ----------- | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `--color-bg-primary`        | `#fff`      | `#000`      | Page background, and the text of a toast                                                                                                             |
| `--color-bg-secondary`      | `#f4f4f4`   | `#141414`   | Header and raised surfaces. The browser toolbar matches it                                                                                           |
| `--color-content-primary`   | `#000`      | `#fff`      | Text and icons                                                                                                                                       |
| `--color-content-secondary` | `#595959`   | `#a3a3a3`   | Secondary text, and the thumb of a scrollbar                                                                                                         |
| `--color-border`            | `#000`      | `#fff`      | Structural borders (ink), and the fill of a toast                                                                                                    |
| `--color-border-subtle`     | `#d4d4d4`   | `#2e2e2e`   | Hairline dividers                                                                                                                                    |
| `--color-accent`            | `#404040`   | `#d4d4d4`   | The interface hue: links, selection, primary action (gray by default, see Accent)                                                                    |
| `--color-bg-accent`         | accent      | accent      | Fill behind a primary action                                                                                                                         |
| `--color-content-on-accent` | `#fff`      | `#000`      | Text on that fill                                                                                                                                    |
| `--color-border-focus`      | accent      | accent      | Focus ring                                                                                                                                           |
| `--color-danger`            | `#c81e1e`   | `#f87171`   | Errors and destructive actions only                                                                                                                  |
| `--color-shadow`            | `#0000002e` | `#ffffff24` | The soft shadow around a list that opens over the page (`--shadow-popup`) and under the header and the bottom bar while content scrolls beneath them |

Rules:

- One accent at a time and one danger color. There are no success or warning colors: use text, an icon or position instead. A new color needs a reason and an update to this table.
- Components use only the semantic `--color-*` tokens, never raw values. Raw values live in `tokens.css`.
- Never convey meaning by color alone.
- What someone owes or is owed is not an error and has no color: the words say it ("You owe", "owes you", "Nothing to settle"), and `--color-danger` stays for errors and for what deletes.
- Contrast follows WCAG 2 AA: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for focus rings and the marks of a chart. Structural borders are ink, 18:1 or more. Two decorations are not held to 3:1, because something else says what they frame (owner decision, 2026-10-29):
    - The hairline `--color-border-subtle`, 1.4 to 1.6:1 in both themes. It draws disabled notches, quiet notices, the Delete notch, the chart grid and the rules of the numbers table; the text names each box.
    - The ink notch on an accent fill (primary button, checked option of a choice group, pressed toggle), 1.5 to 2.4:1 in dark and 2.0 to 4.2:1 in light. The fill, at 4.5:1 or more against the page, is what marks the control.

    Every other pair in the table was checked on 2026-10-25 (text on a background: 4.5:1 at worst; ink on background: 18:1 or more). Check again when a value changes.

- Category colors have their own rules (see Category colors).

### Accent

The user picks the accent from a fixed list, as in `spa-anicalendar`: `data-accent` on `<html>` selects a block that redefines `--color-accent` and the tokens built on it. Gray is the default, so it is the value the tokens start with and needs no block. The settings call the section "App color" ("Color de la app"): "accent" means nothing to most people. Each preset has its own light and dark tone, tuned by hand and checked against both surfaces (`--color-bg-primary` and `--color-bg-secondary`).

| Preset           | Light     | Dark      |
| ---------------- | --------- | --------- |
| `gray` (default) | `#404040` | `#d4d4d4` |
| `amber`          | `#9a5b00` | `#f0a830` |
| `blue`           | `#1d4ed8` | `#8aa4ff` |
| `green`          | `#15803d` | `#4ade80` |
| `violet`         | `#6d28d9` | `#c4b5fd` |
| `pink`           | `#be185d` | `#f9a8d4` |

- Every accent reaches 4.5:1 as text on both surfaces, and its on-accent text (white in light, black in dark) reaches 5:1 on its own fill. Checked on 2026-10-25.
- There is no red preset: it would read as the danger color.
- The accent never changes the browser toolbar color, which follows the header surface.
- The choice lives in `localStorage` (`tarja:accent`) and is applied with the theme before the first paint (see Themes). The settings screen holds it as a `ChoiceGroup` next to the theme. Each option is labeled with its name, not just a swatch.

### Category colors

Every category has a color and an icon (see Icons). The color only helps to find a category at a glance: the icon and the name always come with it, so no meaning depends on color alone.

| Key          | Light     | Dark      | Default category |
| ------------ | --------- | --------- | ---------------- |
| `vermillion` | `#d55e00` | `#d55e00` | Food             |
| `green`      | `#009e73` | `#009e73` | Groceries        |
| `purple`     | `#c86ea0` | `#cc79a7` | Leisure, Treats  |
| `blue`       | `#0072b2` | `#0072b2` | Bills            |
| `orange`     | `#b87f00` | `#e69f00` | Housing, Gifts   |
| `sky`        | `#1c93d7` | `#56b4e9` | Transport        |
| `yellow`     | `#978e0c` | `#f0e442` | Health, Personal |
| `gray`       | `#767676` | `#767676` | Other            |

- Seven of them are the Okabe-Ito set, designed for color-vision deficiency; gray is for "Other". Each tone is adjusted per theme to reach 3:1 against both surfaces (3.08:1 at worst in light, 3.55:1 in dark), the WCAG minimum for graphics.
- Tokens are `--color-category-<key>`, defined with `light-dark()`. The database stores the key, never the value, so retuning a tone is a CSS change.
- A category color paints its icon and, in the charts, its segment of the split by category (owner decision, 2026-10-29), and nothing else. It is never used for text, borders or fills behind text.
- Default categories have a fixed color and icon, defined in `core/` next to their ids. There are eleven and the palette has eight colors, with no ninth that color-vision deficiency keeps apart from the others, so three pairs of related categories share one: Treats has Leisure's purple, Personal has Health's yellow and Gifts has Housing's orange, and the icon and the name tell them apart, as they already do for the custom categories once every color is taken. Gray stays for Other alone. Custom categories choose one of the eight colors and one icon when created or edited, in a picker behind "Color and icon", two `ChoiceGroup`s of swatches: each color is drawn on the current icon and each icon in the current color, and the chosen one is filled with the accent like any other choice, its icon turning one color, so the preview next to the name (in the form that adds a category and in the one that edits it) is what shows the color chosen; the default is the least used by the user's own categories among the seven that stand out (the first one on ties, never gray) and the generic icon.
- Amber (an accent the user can pick), orange and yellow sit close in hue. It is acceptable because the accent never paints an icon and every icon comes with its name; check it on screen and rebalance a tone if they get confused.

### Themes

Three settings: light (the default), dark and system, which follows the OS.

- Tokens are defined once with `light-dark()`. `:root` has `color-scheme: light`, which is the default look; `data-theme="dark"` on `<html>` forces dark and `data-theme="system"` sets `color-scheme: light dark`, so the OS preference applies. Once the app runs the theme is always written on `<html>`, light included.
- The default is no longer what the OS says, so a small inline script at the top of `index.html` copies the saved `tarja:theme` and `tarja:accent` to `<html>` before the first paint: whoever chose dark, system or another accent doesn't see the defaults flash. It also sets the browser toolbar color (below) before the first paint. It is the only inline script, and its keys are those of `src/lib/theme` and `src/lib/accent`.
- A theme change reveals the new theme as ink drifting in from where it was pressed (ADR-0022, see Motion); where the browser has no view transitions, or with reduced motion, `withColorTransition` fades the colors instead of snapping: it sets `data-color-transition` on `<html>` for `--duration-slow`.
- The browser toolbar color (`<meta name="theme-color">`) mirrors `--color-bg-secondary`, the header surface. `index.html` carries one per OS preference, above the inline script. For an explicit theme (light, also when nothing was saved, and dark) the inline script points them all at that theme's header surface before the first paint, so a light app on a dark phone never opens under a dark toolbar; the app keeps them there and puts them back for "system".
- The web app manifest (`vite.config.ts`) holds a single `theme_color` and `background_color` and can't follow the theme: the dark variant (`color_scheme_dark`) is still a proposal that Chrome on Android doesn't read, and an installed app (a WebAPK) keeps the colors it was installed with until Chrome updates it. `background_color` has the light value of `--color-bg-primary`, as light is the default, so an installed app set to dark opens on a light splash screen (Android draws it from the manifest) until the page paints.
- On Android the status bar of an installed app follows the meta tags, but the navigation bar at the bottom takes the manifest's `theme_color`. That one is the dark value of `--color-bg-secondary` (`#141414`), the owner's choice: the bar stays dark in the dark theme and is not far off in the light one. The toolbar still follows the meta tags. A change to it reaches a phone only after the app is reinstalled or Chrome updates it.
- The choice lives in `localStorage` (`tarja:theme`). The settings screen (`/settings`, linked from the header) holds it, next to the language. Both are a `ChoiceGroup`: a radio group drawn as boxes, the checked one filled with the accent. The theme lists Light and Dark first and System last, since System follows the device and is not a look of its own.

## 3. Shape

- **No rounded corners.** Never use `border-radius`; there are no radius tokens. This includes buttons, inputs, avatars and focus outlines.
- **Borders carry the structure**; the only shadow is the soft one around a list that opens over the page. `--color-border` (ink) for structure, `--color-border-subtle` for dividers. Dividers are 1 px (`--size-px`); the notch and emphasis use `--size-1`.
- **Form controls** draw their notch as they are used ([ADR-0023](../docs/adr/0023-buttons-and-fields-draw-their-notch-as-used.md)). A field is a `--color-bg-secondary` box, no radius, 3.5 rem tall, drawn by `Field` (`base.css` only resets the controls: no edge, no fill, the page font): at rest its bottom edge alone, half the left edge on hover, the whole L with the focus, and the label inside it (see `Field`). A button rests as its bottom edge too and grows the left one as it is hovered and pressed; the primary action fills with the accent (`--color-bg-accent`) and a destructive one takes `--color-danger` for its text.
- **The notch.** The tally-stick cut: every box (form control, button, card, the rows of a list) has a thick ink border (`--size-1`) on the left and at the bottom, and none on top or on the right. The exceptions are the `Button` boxes and the fields, which rest as their bottom edge alone and grow the left one ([ADR-0023](../docs/adr/0023-buttons-and-fields-draw-their-notch-as-used.md), see `Button` and `Field`), the ghost button and the expenses of a day, which sit on paper (below), and the square of the "+" in the phone bar, which stands inside the edge of the bar. Without an outline, a box that takes input sits on `--color-bg-secondary` so it still reads as a field. The focus ring is the exception: a full accent outline around the box. Messages (status, notices, errors) keep a thick left border only, in ink, or in `--color-danger` for errors; a quiet notice uses the hairline. The `Toast` is the exception: it is filled with ink. Both edges are `--size-1` (4 px), checked on screen on the form, the lists and the choice groups. A primary button keeps the ink notch on its accent fill, which is what marks it (§2, Palette).
- **Room around the notch.** A box that holds a label has `--size-4` before the text and `--size-12` after it, so its bottom edge runs on past the text like the tail of an L; buttons share it. The options of a `ChoiceGroup` and a `block` button are the exception: their width comes from the row, so they keep `--size-4` before the text and only `--size-2` after it. Boxes in a row are `--size-4` apart, wrapped or not, so each notch stands on its own (the options of a `ChoiceGroup` are the exception: they are one joined box); rows stacked in a list are `--size-2` apart. A link that does what a button does takes the class `.button` and looks like one. A box that holds only a glyph (the arrows of the month) keeps its padding even. The swatches of the color and icon pickers are cells of a `ChoiceGroup`, so they are joined and fill their row.
- **Buttons on their own row.** On the settings screen and under the filters of the list, a button that stands alone in its section is a `block` one: it takes the whole width of the column, so its bottom edge runs from side to side as the tail of its L. Two actions of the same weight side by side (Your data) are two equal `block` boxes while a label fits in each, `--size-4` apart, and when it doesn't they stack, each the whole width. Both cases follow one rule: boxes that sit together share a width.
- **Tabs.** The row of tabs over the Expenses screen, while groups are pinned to it ([ADR-0021](../docs/adr/0021-pinned-groups.md), [the expense list](../docs/specs/expense-list.md)), has no notch and no rule: words in a row, the current one in ink with the `--size-1` ink bar under it, as the current word of the header, the others in secondary text. It never wraps; a long name is cut with an ellipsis.
- **No L inside an L, nor on paper.** The notch marks what stands alone on the page. An action that sits inside another edge or on paper is a ghost (`Button`): text only, underlined, with no box, like the Undo of a toast or the actions under an open expense. The ghost keeps the height of `--size-touch`, so it is as easy to press as a box.
- **Focus ring.** A full accent outline around the box (`--focus-width`, 2 px), `--focus-offset` (2 px) clear of it. A field draws it round its box only when the focus came from the keyboard: a text field claims `:focus-visible` on a tap too, so the root says how the focus last moved (`data-focus-source`, `keyboard` after Tab, `pointer` after a press, `useFocusSource`), and a tap shows the L alone. Where a parent clips it, as the options of a `ChoiceGroup` and the rows of a `Select` list do, it is drawn inside the box instead, `--focus-inset` from the edge: the 4 px of the thick edge plus the width of the ring, so it never covers the notch.
- **The ticket.** The expenses of a group (a day, a week, a month or a category) sit on a `Sheet` (see Components), like a receipt. The rows have no notch and no rule between them: the name and the amount are joined by a leader of dots in `--color-content-secondary`. At the foot come a dashed rule and the line Total, in bold, with its own leader, over a double rule (`--size-1` thick, `--color-border`) that closes the sheet. The leader is a string of dots (`--leader-dots`, one monospace character each) cut where it meets the amount, with an empty alternative text so assistive technology skips it; it disappears when the name takes the whole line, as a long description wraps. A row that opens shows its actions right under it, as ghost buttons, with the room left under them before the next row. The categories screen has the same sheets: the person's own categories, whose rows open with Edit and Delete, and the built-in ones, which have nothing to open, each on a sheet of its own with its label and no foot. So has the ledger of a group, one sheet with no label for the entries in the order they were added or a day on each sheet when read by date; in a closed group its rows have nothing to open and keep the room of the chevron, so the amounts still line up with the total.

### Components

The rules above live in shared components, in `src/components/<name>/`. A screen uses them instead of styling raw elements again; they add no rule of their own. Storybook (`design-system/`) shows each one with its variants and states; the rules stay in this document.

- **`Notice`**: a message set apart by a thick left edge. It is a paragraph that holds only the text. `tone` is `info` (ink, the default), `danger` (`--color-danger`, for errors) or `quiet` (a hairline and secondary text, for what is only background, like an empty list). `role` is `alert` for an error that shows up after an action and `status` for a line that changes while the person looks at it; text that was always there has none.
- **`Button`**: a box with the notch that is drawn as it is used: at rest only its bottom edge (`--size-1`, ink), the left edge grows from the bottom over `--duration-base` to half the height while the pointer is over it (only where there is a hover, `@media (hover: hover)`) and to the whole height while it is pressed, which makes the L; a toggle that is on (`pressed`) keeps the whole L, and a disabled box never draws the left edge. On a touch screen a button at rest is therefore its bottom line alone. `variant` is the default, `primary` (the accent fill, for the main action of a form) or `danger` (both edges and the text in `--color-danger`, for what deletes; the primary gets a darker fill on hover). `ghost` takes the box away: the text underlined, with no padding at the sides so it lines up with the text around it, no notch and no fill, for an action inside another edge or on paper (the Undo of a toast; Edit, Duplicate and Delete under an open expense). It goes with the default and `danger` variants (a `danger` ghost keeps the text in `--color-danger`), never with `primary` or `pressed`. Hovering it thickens the underline, and a disabled ghost loses it. `block` makes the box as wide as its row (see "Buttons on their own row"). `glyph` is for a box that holds only a symbol: even padding, and an `aria-label` is required, on a `ButtonLink` too. `pressed` fills it with the accent and sets `aria-pressed`, for a toggle; an `aria-pressed` the caller sets itself wins over it. A disabled button looks the same whatever its variant: no fill, its text in `--color-content-secondary` and its notch in the hairline color. A button is `type="button"` unless it submits. `ButtonLink` is a link that looks like a button, typed like `Link`, and takes the same `variant`, `ghost`, `block` and `glyph`; the ghost one is also the link back in the header of a phone ([header](../docs/specs/header.md)). `base.css` styles buttons and links with `:where()`, so a variant wins whatever order the stylesheets load in.
- **`ChoiceGroup`**: a fieldset with its legend and one radio per option. The options form one joined box: a single thick edge on the left for the whole group, a thick cut under each option and a hairline (`--color-border-subtle`) between neighbours, so a group is one L however many rows it takes. The checked option is filled with the accent. The options share one width and fill their row, as many to a row as fit: the language (two) and the theme (three) take one row, the six app colors take two or three on a phone, and when the widest word of a label doesn't fit twice, the options go one under the other. A row with fewer options has them wider, so it still fills the row. Each option has `--size-4` before the text and `--size-2` after it, and a label of several words that doesn't fit wraps to a second line. The group measures its widest word in characters (`--choice-word`), since the font is monospace. The options keep the order they are given in, so the one that differs goes last (System, after Light and Dark). `swatch` draws the options as cells that hold what they are given (an icon) instead of a word, as wide as `--size-touch` at least and filling the row; each is named by its label for assistive technology, and a checked one takes everything inside it to the text color on the accent. The group clips what spills out of it (the hairline of the first option of a row), so the focus ring sits inside the option instead of around it, clear of the cut under it (see Focus ring).
- **`Field`**: a box that holds the label and the control and, under it, a hint and the error. The box draws its notch as it is used ([ADR-0023](../docs/adr/0023-buttons-and-fields-draw-their-notch-as-used.md)): on `--color-bg-secondary`, 3.5 rem tall, at rest only its bottom edge in ink (`--size-1`); the left edge grows from the bottom to half the height while the pointer is over it (only where there is a hover, `@media (hover: hover)`) and to the whole height while the field has the focus or a `Select` in it is open, which draws the L; with the focus from the keyboard the focus ring goes round the box as well (see Focus ring). An error draws both edges in `--color-danger`; a disabled field is the hairline at the bottom, its label and text in secondary ink. **The label floats**: while the field is empty and idle it sits in the middle of the box where the text goes, in secondary ink; with the focus, a value or the browser's autofill it shrinks (to `--font-size-s`, by `transform`) to the top of the box over `--duration-base`, and the text sits under it. `Field` gives the control a blank placeholder so the box can tell an empty field (`:placeholder-shown`); a `Select` and a date always hold a value, so their label is always up. The label stays a real `<label>` and is never a placeholder; one that doesn't fit the width is cut with an ellipsis. `icon` draws an icon inside the box at its start, in secondary ink, still in the middle of the box whatever the label does (the magnifying glass of the Search), and the label and the text start after it. `actions` puts buttons beside the box, `--size-4` apart, wrapping under it when they don't fit, their bottom edges in line with the box's (Save next to the income). The hint and the error come `--size-1` under the box. Fields in a form are `--size-4` apart, the gap of the form itself. The error is a `Notice` with `role="alert"`, directly under the field it is about, so it is never at the bottom of the form; a message about the form as a whole (a save that failed) goes after the last field, except in a form whose only field is the thing being saved, like the new category, where it goes under that field. The control is an `<input>` or a `Select` that spreads what `Field` gives it: the `id` the label points at, the blank `placeholder`, `aria-describedby` and `aria-invalid`. `FieldBox` is the same box with no label in it, for a control named elsewhere (the part of each person in a shared expense, named by `aria-label`); it is `--size-touch` tall. A `suffix` ends the box with a short text in secondary ink on the line of what is typed, the unit it is in (the `%` of a share by percent); it isn't read out, so the name of the control says the unit too. A `ChoiceGroup` is a fieldset with its own legend and stays outside.
- **`Select`**: a field that picks one option from a list. It is a native `<select>` and the only way to pick one among several in a form, so no feature styles a `<select>` of its own. It goes inside a `Field`, which gives it the `id` its label points at, `aria-describedby` and `aria-invalid`, and `ref` reaches the field, for a screen that puts the focus on it. `disabled` turns the field off, which then looks like any other disabled field. An option has a `value`, a `label` and, if it wants one, an `icon`: when some options have it and others don't, the others keep its place so the names line up, and a list with none leaves no gap. The rules are those of [Select](#select) below.
- **`Card` and `CardList`**: a `Card` is a box with the notch and the padding of a row (`--size-2` above and below, `--size-3` at the sides). A `CardList` stacks rows `--size-2` apart, as a list of rows should be; `flush` puts them edge to edge, the exception for a list whose rows are already far enough apart, like the expenses of a day on their paper (see The ticket). `label` names a list that has no heading of its own, for assistive technology. What goes inside a card is up to the screen. The rows that open, the expenses and the person's own categories, are not `Card`s but `Disclosure` items in a `CardList` on a `Sheet`: their toggle has no notch, since it is on paper, and the panel sits under it.
- **`Sheet`**: a sheet of paper, like a receipt: `--color-bg-secondary` with `--size-4` of padding, closed by a double rule (`--size-1` thick, `--color-border`), under a `label` centered in capitals, in secondary text, over a dashed rule (1 px, `--color-border`); a sheet without a `label` has neither the heading nor the rule, as the ledger of a group in the order they were added. The label is a heading of level 2, or of level 3 with `level`; `labelRef` lets the screen put the focus on it, out of the tab order, when the row that had the focus is gone (a person removed from a group). What goes on it is up to the screen: the expenses of a day, with their total, and the categories (see The ticket). A `Disclosure` on a sheet has no notch on its toggle and no gap under it, and its panel sits right under the toggle with `--size-3` left under it before the next row.
- **`DateRangeFields`**: the From and To fields of a range of days, each a `Field`, side by side while both fit (a date field is never narrower than about 9.5 rem, so a phone narrower than about 365 px in the list, or 350 px in the charts, puts one under the other). A day counts only between 2000 and 2099, because a date field passes through years like 0002 and 0020 while one is typed: whatever doesn't make a day yet stays in the field without reaching the screen's state, and leaving the field puts the last good day back. `allowEmpty` lets a field be cleared, for a range that may be open at one end. `invalid` and `describedBy` tie both fields to a message the screen shows once, for both of them.
- **`MonthSwitch`**: the name of a month between two arrow buttons (previous and next, `glyph` `Button`s of at least `--size-touch`), `--size-4` from the name at least, with the first letter of the name in capital. It is for the screens that show one month at a time, the Summary and the budgets; the month is the state of the screen, never part of the URL.
- **`Toast`**: a message that goes away by itself and offers one action when there is something to do about it: for the result of something the person can take back (Undo after Delete), with the action, and for a warning that needs no answer (a budget crossed, see [budgets](../docs/specs/budgets.md)), without one. Errors stay `Notice`s. It floats at the bottom, centered in the content column and `--size-4` from the edges and above the bottom safe area: a flat box filled with ink (`--color-border`) and its text in `--color-bg-primary`, so it stands apart from the tab bar and the sheets, which are on `--color-bg-secondary`, and from the page, and is noticed when it comes in. It is the one message that is filled, because it appears on its own: it has no notch and no left edge, and the time left is the fill itself: over the 8 s the ink drains from the right toward the left, leaving a lighter tone of it (a mix of the ink and the page) behind, so what is left is the ink that is still there ([ADR-0019](../docs/adr/0019-the-toast-drains.md)). Both tones keep the text at 7:1 or more. It takes the whole width between its margins, the whole column from 40 rem, with the message at the left and the actions at the right. It holds, in front of the message, a bare `check` icon in the color of its text when it reports something done (no box, close to the message; a warning has none), then the message and, when it has an action, a ghost `Button` in the color of its text. The focus ring inside it is in the color of the page too, since the accent would not show on the ink. A toast that goes away by itself ends with a dismiss button, a `glyph` ghost `Button` with the `close` icon, named by the `dismissLabel` that `ToastProvider` takes by props, which does what Escape does, so a touch screen can clear it away to see what is under it; a pinned toast has none, since its answers close it. One of the kind at a time: a new one replaces the one showing. A **pinned** toast is the same box for a message that has to be answered, so it stays until it is: the notice that a new version is ready, with Update and Later as ghost `Button`s. It is all ink, with nothing draining and no count, Escape doesn't close it, and it sits above the one that goes away by itself, so an Undo never covers it. Only another pinned toast, or taking it away, replaces it. It stays 8 s; hovering it or keeping the focus inside pauses the count, and the drain with it, and both go on with what was left. Escape closes it, unless a control that uses Escape (an open category list) took the key. `ToastProvider` sits in the root layout, so a toast survives a change of screen, and its `role="status"` region is always in the page, so a message that comes in is announced. It is the last stop in the tab order and never blocks a click outside the box. It comes in and leaves with a fade and a short vertical shift over `--duration-base` (§6).
- **`Banner`**: the notice of something that happened elsewhere, a shared expense somebody added ([notifications](../docs/specs/notifications.md), [ADR-0025](../docs/adr/0025-shared-expenses-notify-the-people-they-concern.md)), shaped like the banner of a system notification. It drops in from the top, over the header and under the top safe area, `--size-4` from the sides, as wide as the content column from 40 rem, centered. Two lines, a title and a message, which together are one button that opens what it is about, and a `glyph` ghost `Button` with the `close` icon named by the `dismissLabel` its provider takes by props. A swipe to the side of more than a third of its width, or Escape while the focus is inside it, dismisses it. It goes away by itself after 8 s (hovering or the focus inside pauses it) and never takes the focus; its `role="status"` region is always in the page. One at a time: what comes while one shows is added to it. It is a box with the notch on `--color-bg-secondary`, the color of the header, with `--shadow-popup` around it, the title in `--font-size-s` and `--color-content-secondary` over the message.
- **`Disclosure`**: a toggle and the panel it opens under it, with `aria-expanded` on the toggle and `aria-controls` whenever the panel is in the page: while it is open, and while a `keepMounted` panel is closed and hidden, but not when a closed panel has been dropped. The panel comes in with a fade and a short vertical shift over `--duration-base`. `keepMounted` keeps the panel in the page, hidden, while it is closed, so what was typed in it survives; such a panel also leaves with the fade and the shift before it drops out of the layout. `chevron` ends the toggle with the `expand_more` icon, a half turn when open. `hint` says what the panel holds after the summary, in secondary ink, while it is closed (the day and the payment under More options of the add form), wrapping under the summary when it doesn't fit. `heading` (`h2` or `h3`) puts the toggle inside a heading, for a disclosure that titles a section; the heading keeps the size of the text around it and the toggle takes its weight. A toggle brought into view (a row taking the focus back) stops `--size-2` from the edge, so its focus ring shows whole.
- **`Dialog`**: a box with the notch, centered over the page, for what has to be read before going on and has nothing to do with the screen under it, or, as a sheet, for a task done apart from it; today the question of deleting the account, with the field where its email is typed to confirm, and the privacy notice, a long text in sections that scrolls inside the dialog. It is the native `<dialog>` opened as a modal, so the page behind it stays still and inert, the focus stays inside it and Escape closes it, unless it is told it can't be dismissed (`dismissible`, off while something is being sent from inside it). A tap or click on the veil outside the box closes it the same way, unless it can't be dismissed or is told to stay (`closeOnOutside` off: a form whose edit closing would lose, Edit, Duplicate, Settle up and the add form, which closing empties ([ADR-0026](../docs/adr/0026-the-add-form-keeps-its-button-in-sight-and-folds-its-options.md)), [ADR-0024](../docs/adr/0024-the-list-filters-open-in-a-sheet.md)). It holds a `title`, an `h2` that names it, and what it is given, `--size-4` apart. Its `actions` go apart under that, `--size-4` from each other, wrapped or not, and are `ghost` `Button`s (or links with the class `button button--ghost`), since there is no L inside an L; they have no padding at the sides, so they line up with the text. The dialog itself takes the focus when it opens, not its first action, so no ring shows on a choice nobody made; the first Tab reaches the first action. Behind it comes a veil of the page's own color at 80 %, so it brings no color of its own. It is at most 32 rem wide and `--size-4` from the edges of a phone, and what doesn't fit in its height scrolls inside it. It takes its copy by props and knows nothing of why it is shown. It comes in and leaves with a fade and a short vertical shift over `--duration-base` (§6). **The sheet variant** (`variant="sheet"`) holds the form of a task done apart from the screen (adding, editing or duplicating an expense, settling up, [ADR-0016](../docs/adr/0016-forms-for-a-task-open-in-a-sheet.md)). Below 40 rem it is anchored to the bottom edge and as wide as the screen, with an ink hairline (`--size-px`) on top instead of the notch, which makes no sense against the screen edges; it is padded by the safe areas, at most `100dvh` less the top inset tall, and scrolls inside. From 40 rem it is the centered dialog above. Its title row can end with a close button (a `glyph` ghost `Button` with the `close` icon, named by `closeLabel`), and it can say where the focus goes when it opens (`initialFocus`; by default the dialog itself). Its `actions` are stuck to its bottom edge, on its own paper, while what it holds scrolls under them, so a long sheet keeps them in sight on a small phone; while what it holds goes on under them they cast the shadow of the bottom bar upward, gone once the sheet is scrolled to its end ([ADR-0018](../docs/adr/0018-a-shadow-where-content-scrolls-under-an-edge.md)). The actions of a sheet that holds a form may hold the form's main button, a box (the Add of the add form, [ADR-0026](../docs/adr/0026-the-add-form-keeps-its-button-in-sight-and-folds-its-options.md)); every other action stays a ghost. **The page variant** (`variant="page"`) is the sheet of the add form on a phone ([ADR-0026](../docs/adr/0026-the-add-form-keeps-its-button-in-sight-and-folds-its-options.md)): below 40 rem it takes the whole screen, with no edge of its own, comes up from below like the sheet, and its title row, on its paper and edge to edge over the padding of the page, is stuck to the top (past the top safe area) while what it holds scrolls under it, casting the shadow of the header once something is under it. The row starts with the way back, a `glyph` ghost `Button` with `chevron_left` named by `closeLabel`, which closes it as the × does, and ends with `headAction`, the main action of its form (the add form puts a ghost `check` glyph named Add or Save there). From 40 rem a page is the centered dialog. The sheet of the list filters has no × but a Close action at the right end of its actions ([ADR-0024](../docs/adr/0024-the-list-filters-open-in-a-sheet.md)). Below 40 rem, while any dialog is open, the page behind does not scroll. It stays in the page while closed, so what was typed in it survives.

- **`Checkbox`**: a square box with a label, for a yes or no in a list (who is in a shared expense, the simplified debts). The box has the notch and sits on `--color-bg-secondary` like any field; checked, it fills with the accent and shows a check in `--color-content-on-accent`, drawn by the shared `Icon` set. The whole row (box and label) is the target and at least `--size-touch` tall; a native `<input type="checkbox">` underneath keeps the keyboard, the focus ring (drawn around the box, `--focus-offset` clear of it) and the screen reader. `hint` goes under the label like the hint of a `Field`. Several in a list are `--size-2` apart.
- **`BottomNav`**: the tab bar of a phone ([bottom navigation](../docs/specs/bottom-navigation.md)). Fixed at the bottom on `--color-bg-secondary`, with an ink hairline on top and the bottom safe area as padding; equal links, each an icon over its word at `--font-size-s`, `--bottom-nav-height` (3.75rem) tall. The current link carries `aria-current="page"` and a bar of `--size-1` in ink along its top edge, with no fill and no accent. It takes its items by props, reads no messages, and is hidden from 40 rem up and, by sliding out of the tab order, while a text field or a `Select` outside a dialog has the focus. It can hold one `action`: the "+" that adds an expense ([ADR-0017](../docs/adr/0017-the-look-of-the-plus.md)), in the middle of five equal columns. It is drawn like the links, an icon over a word: a flat square of `--size-8` in the accent fill (`--color-bg-accent`, the icon in `--color-content-on-accent`) with a thickened `add` icon centered in it, over its bold word. The square has no notch, border or radius, the one box without a notch; the whole column is the button, `--bottom-nav-height` tall, named by its label, which holds the word that shows; it has no `aria-current`.

## 4. Typography and icons

### Typography

- Everything is set in **IBM Plex Mono** (SIL OFL 1.1), amounts included: monospace lines the digits up and fits the ledger feeling. It comes from `@fontsource/ibm-plex-mono`, the latin subset (which covers the accents, ñ, ¿, ¡ and €) in weights 400 and 700, about 15 KB each. It is served by the app itself and precached, so it works offline; `font-display: swap` shows the fallback while it loads.
- Stack: `'IBM Plex Mono', ui-monospace, 'Cascadia Mono', 'Menlo', 'Consolas', monospace`.
- Form controls inherit the page font and its 16 px size.
- **Type scale**: a standard modular scale, minor third (×1.2) from a 1 rem base. `--font-size-s` is 0.833 rem (secondary text, like the hint under a heading), `--font-size-m` 1 rem (body and form controls), `--font-size-l` 1.2 rem (page titles) and `--font-size-xl` 1.44 rem (the sign-in title). The logo is sized apart (`--font-size-logo`). Form controls never go below `--font-size-m`, so iOS doesn't zoom. Hierarchy also uses weight (700) and case, never a size outside the scale.
- **The logo**: the name _Tarja_ is set in the regular weight of Dot Matrix (Svein Kåre Gunnarson, Dionaea Fonts), whose readme says "the fonts are freeware". The bold came first; the owner asked to try the regular one, so the weight was seen on Android, where it reads well, and is still to see on iOS (thin at `--font-size-l` in the header, better large, `--font-size-logo`, 3 rem, on the sign-in screen). Only the letters of the name ship, as a woff2 subset of about 1.5 KB; the original TTFs aren't in the repo, and `src/assets/fonts/NOTICE.md` credits the author and records how the subset was made. It is for the name only, never for text: a dot matrix face doesn't read small. Its terms say nothing about redistribution, so the subset stays limited to those letters. The `Brand` component sets it. In the prerelease build ([ADR-0015](../docs/adr/0015-cloudflare-deploys-and-a-prerelease-worker.md)) the name is followed by `-pre` at half its size and in the body font, since the subset has only the letters of the name and a dot matrix face doesn't read small. The icon of that build is the same drawing with the diagonal line red (`#ff6b6b`) instead of blue (`#8aa4ff`), in `public/prerelease/`.

### Icons

- **Material Symbols, Sharp style** (Apache 2.0): square ends and corners, like the rest of the identity. Weight 400, 24 px grid, shown at `1.5rem`.
- Only the icons in use are copied into the repo as inline SVG (a few hundred bytes each), with the license notice. No icon font and no package at runtime.
- Icons are decorative (`aria-hidden`): the name always comes with them. They take `currentColor`, or the color of their category.
- The default categories use `restaurant`, `shopping_cart`, `theater_comedy` (Leisure), `receipt_long`, `home`, `directions_bus`, `medical_services`, `checkroom` (Personal), `sports_esports` (Treats), `redeem` (Gifts) and `more_horiz`. A custom category with no pick uses `category`.
- The icons a custom category can choose from (Material Symbols names), grouped here only for reading: `restaurant`, `local_cafe`, `local_bar`, `fastfood`, `bakery_dining`; `shopping_cart`, `shopping_bag`, `storefront`, `checkroom`, `redeem`; `sports_esports`, `movie`, `music_note`, `sports_soccer`, `fitness_center`, `theater_comedy`, `beach_access`; `directions_bus`, `directions_car`, `local_gas_station`, `train`, `flight`, `two_wheeler`; `home`, `bolt`, `water_drop`, `wifi`, `receipt_long`, `build`, `bed`; `medical_services`, `local_pharmacy`, `spa`; `pets`, `school`, `child_care`, `work`; `savings`, `payments`, `credit_card`, `account_balance`, `subscriptions`; `volunteer_activism`, `more_horiz`, `category`. The stored value is the name; the picker shows them in this order.
- The interface has icons of its own, kept out of that list (`UI_ICONS` in `src/components/icon/icon-paths.ts`). `expand_more` is the chevron of a disclosure, the "can be pressed" icon: it ends every row that opens a panel, the expenses and the person's own categories. It is not a pencil: a pencil would promise Edit, which is only one of the actions in the panel. `chevron_right` ends a row that leads to another screen instead, as a group does in the list of groups, and `chevron_left` starts the link back in the header of a phone. `check` is the mark of a `Checkbox`. `filter_list` is the Filters button of the expense list ([ADR-0024](../docs/adr/0024-the-list-filters-open-in-a-sheet.md)). `keep` (a pin, outlined) and `keep_filled` (the same pin filled) are the pin of a group in the list of groups: outlined while it isn't pinned to Expenses, filled while it is ([ADR-0021](../docs/adr/0021-pinned-groups.md)). For the shared expenses, `groups` and `person` are a group and a friend in the lists and in the `Select` of the form, and `bar_chart` and `settings` two of the tabs of the bottom bar, the other two using `receipt_long` and `groups`. A category can't take any of them, and neither the picker nor the server accepts one. `Icon` draws both kinds. `search`, a magnifying glass, is the icon inside the Search of the expenses. `close` is the only one drawn by CSS instead of `Icon`: a mask of the × of the search field (`expense-list-controls.css`).

## 5. Layout and mobile-first

- Base CSS targets phones. Widen with `@media (width >= 40rem)` and `(width >= 64rem)`, never with `max-width` queries.
- The content column is capped at 40 rem and centered on larger screens.
- Touch targets are at least `--size-touch` (44 px).
- `viewport-fit=cover` is on, so anything anchored to an edge pads itself with `env(safe-area-inset-*)`.
- Use `dvh`, not `vh`, for full-height blocks.
- Form controls use 16 px or more, so iOS doesn't zoom in on focus.
- Below 40 rem the destinations are a bar at the bottom ([`BottomNav`](#components), [ADR-0012](../docs/adr/0012-shared-expenses.md)); the page leaves room for it, and the toast and anything else anchored to the bottom sit above the bar and the safe area. From 40 rem the header holds the words. A screen deeper than a tab also has a link back to its parent at the left of the header, below 40 rem only (a ghost `ButtonLink` with a chevron and the name of the place, [header](../docs/specs/header.md)); the logo stays in the middle. The bar holds the "+" that adds an expense, in the middle; from 40 rem there is no floating button: the header holds the ghost button "Add expense" next to Expenses ([header](../docs/specs/header.md)), before the logo in the tab order.
- **The header scrolls away and comes back.** It is sticky (`--header-height` tall, plus the top safe area, which is also what `scroll-padding-top` of the page reserves under it), slides out on a scroll down and back in on a scroll up, and never leaves the tab order: the rules are in [header](../docs/specs/header.md). The bottom bar stays.
- Layout still needs a check on a real iOS Safari and Android Chrome (`TODO.md`).

### Scrollbars

- **With a mouse** (`pointer: fine`) the browser draws a classic scrollbar that takes room from the page: the content jumps sideways between a screen that scrolls and one that doesn't, and when a dialog sets `overflow: hidden`. So the page keeps the room (`scrollbar-gutter: stable` on the root, which also keeps it while a dialog hides the scrollbar), and the bar is thin (`scrollbar-width: thin`, on every scroll box, the open list of a `Select` included, which keeps no room of its own) with a thumb of `--color-content-secondary` and no track (`scrollbar-color`), so it reads as a thin line in the margin, the nearest a page can get to the bars of a phone.
- **On a touch screen it is left alone**: the system draws its own, over the content and only while it scrolls. The rules are inside `@media (pointer: fine)` in `base.css`, so a phone never reads them.
- A browser that doesn't know one of the three properties draws its own bar, which is fine; none is faked. Under forced colors the browser takes the properties back. The bar is never hidden (`scrollbar-width: none`): a box that scrolls sideways, like the chart, or a list in a dialog, would give a mouse nothing to show that it does.
- Not done, on purpose: a bar drawn by script that floats over the content. A classic bar can't be made to overlay in CSS, so only a script could, and it would have to redo dragging, keyboard and assistive technology and keep a second source of truth for the scroll position.

### Select

- A `Select` is a native `<select>`, so the keyboard, the screen readers and the phones handle it as they handle any select: arrow keys, letters to jump to a name, Escape to close, and the list of the system where the browser keeps its own. Every field that picks one option from a list (the category in the expense form and in the filters of the charts and of the list, the payment, the installments, the groups and the people) is one.
- **The field** is the same in every browser: the box of its `Field` (see `Field`), the chevron at the end (it turns over while the list is open, where the browser says so), and, when the options have icons, the icon of the chosen option before its name. That icon is drawn over the field, not by the select, so it shows also where the list can't draw one.
- **The list where the browser lets a select be styled** (`appearance: base-select`): a box with the notch on `--color-bg-primary`, as wide as the field, with a row per option at least `--size-touch` tall, each with its icon and its name, and it scrolls past half the screen (`dvh`). It shows whole rows only, with `--size-2` free above and under them: its height is the most rows that fit in that room, and scrolling stops on a row (`scroll-snap`), so no row is ever cut by its edges, by a finger, a wheel or the keys. The browser places it under the field, or above when there is more room there, and over every box that clips, like the sheet of a form. It has a soft shadow (`--shadow-popup`) all round, so it stands out from the page, and it comes in with a fade over `--duration-base`. The row under the pointer or with the focus is on `--color-bg-secondary`, the chosen one is in bold, with no check mark and no change of background, and the row the keys are on has the focus ring as well (see Focus ring).
- **Elsewhere** the list is the system's own and shows the names alone. That is the price of a native select, taken on purpose: it behaves as the person expects on their phone, and it needs no code to place the list, follow the keys or close it.
- The color of an icon alone says nothing, so the name is always there. A row without an icon (the "All categories" of the charts and of the list) keeps the place of one, so the names line up.
- Only one option is picked at a time: no `multiple` select. A desktop browser draws one as a box of rows picked with Control or Command, which nobody guesses, and no screen needs one today; a filter that takes several would be a set of `Checkbox`es.
- `CategorySelect` (in the categories feature) is a `Select` whose options are categories, each with its `CategoryBadge` as the icon.

## 6. Motion

- Animated transitions are part of the identity: things enter, leave and change with a transition.
- Always use `--duration-fast`, `--duration-base` and `--duration-slow` (120, 200 and 360 ms), `--duration-reveal` (1080 ms, only for the theme change) and `--ease-standard`, `--ease-enter` and `--ease-exit`. Never literal values.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` zeroes the durations in one place (`tokens.css`). Components don't special-case it, except for the drain of the Toast (below).
- Prefer animating `transform` and `opacity`.
- **The theme change** is an ink drift: the new theme is shown through soft-edged blobs that grow from the one point where it was pressed, drifting apart a little as they grow to make the edge lumpy and back together at the end, (the top right corner for a change made with the keyboard) until they cover the screen, over `--duration-reveal` with `--ease-standard`. It is a view transition of the whole page whose new picture is masked; the mask is driven by a registered custom property (`--theme-progress`), and the ink has no color of its own, it is the new theme. The header, the bottom bar and the underline give up their own transition names while it runs, so they are part of the picture. The browser gives the page no pointer events while it runs, so a tap ends it at once and is repeated on what is under it. A change that comes before the last ink has had its time fades the colors instead of starting another ink. With reduced motion, or no view transitions, the colors fade over `--duration-slow` instead. A change between two themes that look the same, as System on a dark device and Dark, has nothing to reveal and just changes.
- **Routes slide horizontally.** What moves is the picture of the screen as it is seen (the `root` of the view transition), never the page box, which can be far taller than the screen and scrolled: its picture would be cut, blurred or carried up and down. The header and the bottom bar hold still over it under their own names, and the underline slides on its own. All of them run over `--duration-slow` with `--ease-standard`. A slide never holds a tap back: the browser sends every tap during a transition to the root, so a tap ends the running transition at once and is repeated on what is under it, and a second link pressed while one slides opens without waiting. The header and the bottom bar show only their new picture, never a fade between two sizes.
    - **Between sections** (Expenses, Groups, Summary and Settings, in the order of the bar, and every screen under them) the two screens move as one strip: going right along the bar, the new one pushes the old one out to the left; going left, the other way round (the `next` and `previous` types). The tabs of the pinned groups in the title of Expenses (ADR-0021) slide the screen the same way, along their row: the row of tabs holds still under its own name (`pinned-tabs`), never moving from where it was, and only the mark changes word.
    - **Deeper in the same section** the new screen is laid over the old one from the right, while the old one moves a third of the way left and dims to 60 %; going back, the top screen is taken off to the right and the one under it comes back (the `forward` and `back` types). Each route has a depth: `/` is 0, `/settings`, `/summary` and `/groups` 1, `/categories`, `/charts`, `/budgets` and a group 2, the settings of a group 3. The link back in the header of a phone leads to a shallower screen, so it slides back with no motion of its own.
    - **Two screens of one section at the same depth** (the charts and the budgets) only fade: the old one out over `--duration-fast`, the new one in over `--duration-base` (the `level` type).
    - With reduced motion the durations are zero, so the screen just changes.
- **Fields.** The left edge of a field grows from the bottom (`scaleY`) over `--duration-base` with `--ease-standard`: to half on hover, whole with the focus. The label goes up and shrinks, or comes back down, over `--duration-base` with `--ease-standard`, by `transform` alone; the icon of the box stays still. With reduced motion they just change.
- **The bottom bar** hides while a field has the focus with a shift down and a fade over `--duration-base` (`transform` and `opacity`), and comes back the same way. The mark of the current tab is part of each link and goes with the fade of the page.
- **The header underline** slides to the word of the new section as one piece, stretching to its width, over `--duration-base` with `--ease-standard`, whatever the page does under it. It is a single element with a `view-transition-name` of its own (`nav-underline`), and no script animates it; only the new mark is drawn while it moves. The header reads the screen already shown, not the one being opened, so the underline is still in its old place when the transition takes its first picture.
- **The header** slides up by its own height when the page scrolls down and back when it scrolls up, over `--duration-base` with `--ease-standard`, by `transform` alone. It is never taken out of the page (no `display`, `visibility`, `inert` or `aria-hidden`), so the keyboard still reaches it, and it shows while a control in it has the keyboard focus. With reduced motion it just changes. The bottom bar doesn't move with it.
- **Toast.** It comes in from below with a fade and a short vertical shift over `--duration-base` with `--ease-enter`, and leaves the same way with `--ease-exit`. The ink drains from the right toward the left over the 8 s it stays (`scaleX`, linear), leaving the lighter tone. The 8 s is a timer, not a transition, so it takes no `--duration-*`. With reduced motion the animation is off: the toast stays all ink, and it still goes after 8 s. A pinned toast comes in and leaves the same way and is all ink.
- **Banner.** It comes in from above with a fade and a short vertical shift over `--duration-base` with `--ease-enter`, and leaves the same way with `--ease-exit`; dismissed by a swipe, it follows the finger (`translateX`) and leaves to that side. The 8 s is a timer, not a transition.
- **Dialog.** It comes in with a fade and a short vertical shift over `--duration-base` with `--ease-enter`, and leaves the same way with `--ease-exit`; the veil behind it fades with it. `display` and `overlay` wait for the transition, so the browser doesn't take it out of the top layer before it has faded.
- **Sheet.** Below 40 rem it comes in from the bottom edge (`translateY(100%)` to none) with a fade, over `--duration-base` with `--ease-enter`, and leaves the same way with `--ease-exit`; from 40 rem it moves like the dialog. The veil fades with it, and `display` and `overlay` wait for the transition as in the dialog.
- **Lists.** A new expense enters from above with a fade and a short vertical shift; a deleted one fades out before it leaves. A row that starts to match a search or a filter, or that lands in another group, comes in the same way; one that stops matching just leaves.
- **Disclosures.** The panel under a row comes in with a fade and a short vertical shift, over `--duration-base`. A panel kept in the page, like the form of a new group, leaves the same way, over `--duration-base` with `--ease-exit`, and only then drops out of the layout. The chevron at the end of the row turns half a turn as it opens and turns back as it closes, over `--duration-base` with `--ease-standard`, by `transform`, driven by `aria-expanded` on the toggle.
- **Charts.** Columns grow up from the baseline (`scaleY`, from the bottom) and the segments of the split along from their start (`scaleX`, moved into place by `translateX`), over `--duration-base` with `--ease-standard`, when they come in and on every change of range, grouping or category. The cap of the biggest column rides on its top the same way. The selection, today's frame and the scale change at once.

## 7. CSS and copy conventions

- Plain CSS, in the file of the route or component that uses it. BEM class names, alphabetical properties, no `!important`; Stylelint enforces them. Combine classes with `clsx`.
- Spacing, motion and color come from `tokens.css`.
- UI text is never hard-coded: it goes through i18n (`src/assets/locales`). Everything else in the project is in English.
